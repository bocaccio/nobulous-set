Nobulous Research
Journal (NRJ)

Three games. One terrible idea.

The Journal reveals that Build‑a‑Tyrant (BAT), the Nobulous Apprenticeship Program (NAP) and the Automaton of Shale (AoS) were never separate jobs. NAP supplied the knowledge and the brain, BAT supplied the body, AoS supplied the kidnapping and the cover‑up. What remains is to walk the finished Imposter into the Council chamber and survive nine rounds of scrutiny.

Components

  • 18 NRJ cards for BAT — 6 Experimental Protocols, 6 Unauthorized Modifications, 6 Research Notes
  • 14 NRJ cards for NAP — 4 Following Setbacks, 4 Imposter Tasks, 6 Devious Opportunities
  • 19 NRJ cards for AoS — 6 Nobulous Interference, 6 Corrupted Limitations, 6 Backdoor Routines, 1 Covert Sensor Array
  • 24 Council Test cards, printed as Exhibits
  • From 20 Strong: the 17 unit dice — 4 Purple, 4 Blue, 4 Green, 4 Yellow, 1 Red
  • From 20 Strong: the 3 stat dice — HP, STR, REC — and the Chip
  • Plus the components of whichever games you are playing

What NRJ adds

NRJ has two independent uses. You may use either, or both.

  • Overlay modules. Optional cards that interfere with each standalone game. Three tiers per game.
  • The campaign. Play all three games, carry your results forward, and finish with the Imposter Scenario.
The tiers are not difficulty levels Tier I — Agenda introduces Nobulous’ interference. Tier II — Contamination injects corrupted material into the game’s own decks and is the pressure peak. Tier III — Exploitation gives you tools to exploit the mess. Each tier includes the ones before it.

Nobulous Apprenticeship Program (NAP) module — 14 cards

TierAdd

A difficulty carrying 3 or more keeps the count it asks for; one carrying fewer has none left to give back, so Tier I raises its count to 3. At your difficulty that is: Easy 3 Following and 0 base, Standard 3 and 0, Hard 3 and 1, Nobulous 3 and 2. An unresolved Following Setback moves into the next Time Slot, including Evening to the next Morning. A Following Setback does not lock the row: the restriction printed on the card replaces “no other Assignment may be worked until this Setback is resolved”. It moves before the next reveal and counts toward the receiving row’s 4‑card limit; if that row is full, it stays where it is and tries to follow again at the next slot advance. Resolve an end‑of‑slot effect printed on the card before moving it.
IIAdd the 4 Imposter Tasks, one per subject. Each replaces a Credit Task of the same subject, chosen at random, and inherits that Task’s recovery‑color dot.
IIIReplace the 6 Opportunities with the 6 Devious Opportunities.
Imposter Tasks Imposter Tasks are Mandatory and grant no Credits. You still have to clear them; clearing them advances no subject. Both faces print a recovery reward.

An Imposter Task flips once. Unresolved at the end of a Time Slot, it turns to its Demand side and keeps its stored progress. Unresolved again at the end of its Demand visit, it is discarded and its stored dice exhaust. These four print no Demand penalty, so there is none to pay.

Devious Opportunities bank their help Four of the six pay nothing when you complete them: you keep the card and spend it once during the Final Exam — or discard it during the apprenticeship to ignore one Setback’s restrictions for the rest of the game. That Setback stays in its Time Slot and still has to be resolved; it simply stops charging upkeep and stops restricting what else you may work. The other two resolve immediately. Tier III replaces the base Opportunities, so NAP’s own coupons are not in the deck at this tier: your Opportunity rewards are banked for the exams instead of spent along the way. Spending one is free and costs no dice.

A Devious Opportunity keeps the normal Opportunity lifetime — one rotation for a final chance, in the same Time Slot on the following day.

Corrupted Limitations are revealed before required‑color dice are committed, exactly like native Limitations, and withhold 2 dice of a randomly selected color on top of their printed corruption. A withheld color cannot answer a required‑color dot, exactly as it cannot under a native Limitation.

Playing the campaign

  1. Play at Tier III. The campaign is Tier III only, because the Research Pool is built out of the Tier III cards themselves — see below. Tiers I and II are for playing the three games on their own.
  2. Win BAT, NAP and AoS in any order. If you lose a game, replay that game only — the campaign does not restart.
  3. Each won game hands you the Tier III cards you claimed, up to 3 from NAP and 2 each from BAT and AoS. Those are your Research Pool.
  4. Play the Imposter Scenario.

No written bookkeeping is needed. Physical cards and their printed states carry the whole campaign history, and nothing is taken out of the BAT, NAP or AoS decks — every card the finale uses came from this box.

The Research Pool — how many you carry out of each game

At most 3 out of NAP, 2 out of BAT and 2 out of AoS. Seven cards into the finale, and every one of them takes 2 off the Target of one Exhibit it can answer. They are the Tier III cards you claimed, so a cap is a ceiling: you carry what you actually took.

You claim an Opportunity by working it like an Assignment, in place of a Credit Task. You claim a Research Note or a Backdoor Routine by meeting the condition printed on it and giving up the next Upgrade a completed Experiment earns, or the next Program Reward.

A card you carry is retained; lay the retained cards face‑up, grouped by game. If you claimed more than you may carry, take any that many — every retained card of a game is worth the same and answers the same domain. Whether you spent one during its own game makes no difference: nothing on the table records that, and a campaign can sit for weeks between games. One you spend in the finale is gone for the rest of it.

Each game lays out 6 Research Notes in BAT, 6 Devious Opportunities in NAP and 6 Backdoor Routines in AoS, so whatever you did not carry comes to the finale as candidates — a card you claimed and could not carry among them. Shuffle them into one face‑down Candidate deck with a discard pile beside it. A candidate answers nothing.

The Imposter Scenario

The Council has assembled a Dossier on you. Each card in it is an Exhibit — one thing they intend to ask about. You cannot refuse it and you cannot outrun it; you can only decide how much of it to face at a time, and you have nine sessions before the Council rules.

  1. Deal 5 Knowledge, 5 Physical and 5 Cover‑Up Exhibits at random from the 24‑card Council Test library. Shuffle those 15 into a face‑down Dossier.
  2. Each Exhibit prints its Target, its Damage and a Reward. Only five of the Rewards do anything, and those five are in bold; the rest are the Council being gracious, and are set in italics to say so. Nothing else on the card is used.
  3. Choose one Imposter card and one of its two sides; the ability is printed on the card. The Imposter begins at 5 HP / 2 STR / 3 REC with all 17 dice, less however many the difficulty you chose starts exhausted.
  4. Keep the retained cards face‑up near the table and the Candidate deck face‑down beside them, with room for a discard pile.
  5. Play 9 sessions.
You win by clearing the Dossier, and you lose two ways Win the moment the Dossier is empty and no Exhibit is left standing. Lose if the ninth session ends with any Exhibit still in the Dossier or on the table, or if your Health ever reaches 0. Either way you replay the finale, not the campaign.

The five Imposters

Choose one Imposter — The Archivist, The Understudy, The Usher, The Porter or The Steward — and one of its two sides. All five begin at 5 HP / 2 STR / 3 REC and differ only in the ability printed on the card.

The Council’s attention

The Council examines one aspect of you at a time, and you can see what they will turn to next. The attention starts on Yellow and advances one step at the end of every session:

Yellow → Green → Blue → Purple → Red → back to Yellow.

You may only apply dice of the attention’s color or the next color on that list. Other dice may still be committed and rolled — they simply cannot be applied, and they exhaust like any other committed die. Every color is therefore usable for two consecutive sessions.

Attention

A session

  1. Deploy. Take as many Exhibits as you like from the top of the Dossier — any number, including none — and place them face‑up in front of you. For each one you deploy, recover 1 exhausted die, or mend 1 Health if nothing is exhausted.
  2. Answer the evidence. Spend Research Pool cards on the Exhibits you just deployed (below). Exhibits deployed in an earlier session can no longer be answered.
  3. Strategy, twice. Commit dice, roll, and apply hits to your standing Exhibits: a hit is 1 success, a Crit is 2, and only the allowed colors may be applied. Then take up the dice that missed and throw again. Nothing is compulsory.
  4. Resolve, in this order:
      1. Every Exhibit still standing deals its Damage to you.
      2. Exhaust every committed die that applied nothing, and every die lying on an Exhibit that has just fallen.
      3. Take the Reward of each Exhibit that fell, if it is one of the five that pay.
      4. Recover up to REC exhausted dice.
      5. Advance the attention and tick the clock.

An Exhibit falls when the successes on it meet or exceed its Target. Dice applied to an Exhibit that is still standing stay on it, and the successes they scored stay with them — progress on an Exhibit is never lost, and those dice are neither available nor exhausted until the card falls.

What the Council lets slip

Every Exhibit prints a Reward and five of them pay. Those five are printed in bold and begin “When this Exhibit falls”; take the Reward at step 3 of Resolve — after the dice on the fallen Exhibit have been exhausted, and before your REC recovery. The other nineteen are printed in italics and do nothing.

Answering an Exhibit

A retained card answers only its own domain, and reduces the Target of one Exhibit you have just deployed. A card you spend is gone. Candidates answer nothing at all.

At most seven retained cards go against fifteen Exhibits — 3 from NAP, 2 from BAT and 2 from AoS. No Exhibit drops below Target 1.

Every Tier III card prints an Imposter clause along the foot. You get it as well as the −2, in the moment you spend the card, and it lasts to the end of that session. All eighteen print one; the eleven you never claimed are Candidates.

Terminology

The Research Pool is what you carried in — the retained cards, at most seven. The Candidate deck is everything you did not carry. The Dossier is the 15 Exhibits. The dice pool is the Imposter’s dice.

Standalone Imposter mode

You can play the finale without the campaign, and without opening another box: every card it needs is in this one. Take the 6 Research Notes, the 6 Devious Opportunities and the 6 Backdoor Routines. Draw at random 3 Opportunities, 2 Notes and 2 Routines as retained — the campaign’s own caps — and shuffle the other eleven into the Candidate deck. Build the 15‑card Dossier normally, pick a difficulty, and play the nine‑session finale. The Imposter clauses come with the cards, so standalone deals you seven at random where the campaign would have let you choose them.
