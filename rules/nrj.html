The Nobulous Set · Campaign Supplement

Nobulous Research
Journal (NRJ)

Three games. One terrible idea.

The Journal reveals that Build‑a‑Tyrant (BAT), the Nobulous Apprenticeship Program (NAP) and the Automaton of Shale (AoS) were never separate jobs. NAP supplied the knowledge and the brain, BAT supplied the body, AoS supplied the kidnapping and the cover‑up. What remains is to walk the finished Imposter into the Council chamber and survive nine rounds of scrutiny.

Components

  • 18 NRJ cards for BAT — 6 Experimental Protocols, 6 Unauthorized Modifications, 6 Research Notes
  • 14 NRJ cards for NAP — 4 Following Setbacks, 4 Impostor Tasks, 6 Devious Opportunities
  • 18 NRJ cards for AoS — 6 Nobulous Interference, 6 Corrupted Limitations, 6 Backdoor Routines
  • 24 Council Test Scenario cards
  • From 20 Strong: the 17 unit dice, the 3 stat dice (Health, Strategy, Recovery) and the Chip
  • Plus the components of whichever games you are playing

What NRJ adds

NRJ has two independent uses. You may use either, or both.

  • Overlay modules. Optional cards that interfere with each standalone game. Three tiers per game.
  • The campaign. Play all three games, carry your results forward, and finish with the Imposter Scenario.
The tiers are not difficulty levels Tier I — Agenda introduces Nobulous’ interference. Tier II — Contamination injects corrupted material into the game’s own decks and is the pressure peak. Tier III — Exploitation gives you tools to exploit the mess. Each tier includes the ones before it.

Playing the campaign

  1. Choose a tier and apply that module to every game you play.
  2. Win BAT, NAP and AoS in any order. If you lose a game, replay that game only — the campaign does not restart.
  3. Each won game contributes 6 cards to the Research Pool.
  4. Play the Imposter Scenario.

No written bookkeeping is needed. Physical cards and their printed states carry the whole campaign history.

The Imposter Scenario

The Council has assembled a Dossier on you. Each card in it is an Exhibit — one thing they intend to ask about. You cannot refuse it and you cannot outrun it; you can only decide how much of it to face at a time, and you have nine sessions before the Council rules.

  1. Deal 5 Knowledge, 5 Physical and 5 Cover‑Up Scenarios at random from the 24‑card Council Test library. Shuffle those 15 into a face‑down Dossier.
  2. An Exhibit’s Target is its printed Target minus 5: a printed 8 is a Target of 3, a 9 is 4, a 10 is 5. Apart from the five Rewards below, nothing else printed on a Council Test is used in this scenario.
  3. An Exhibit’s Damage is 1, or 2 if its Target is 5.
  4. The Imposter begins at 5 HP / 2 STR / 3 REC with all 17 dice available.
  5. Keep the Research Pool face‑up beside the table. Play 9 sessions.
You win by clearing the Dossier, and you lose two ways Win the moment the Dossier is empty and no Exhibit is left standing. Lose if the ninth session ends with any Exhibit still in the Dossier or on the table, or if your Health ever reaches 0. Either way you replay the finale, not the campaign.

The Council’s attention

The Council examines one aspect of you at a time, and you can see what they will turn to next. The attention starts on Yellow and advances one step at the end of every session:

Yellow → Green → Blue → Purple → Red → back to Yellow.

You may only apply dice of the attention’s colour or the next colour on that list. Other dice may still be committed and rolled — they simply cannot be applied, and they exhaust like any other committed die. Every colour is therefore usable for two consecutive sessions, which is what makes recovery a plan rather than a reflex: recover for the colour that is coming, not the one in front of you.

Attention

Session 5 is the hard one: Red and Yellow are the only colours you may apply, and you own exactly one Red die. Session 9 is the kindest, Purple and Red — so the Dossier should be nearly empty well before the Council’s attention comes back around.

A session

  1. Deploy. Take as many Exhibits as you like from the top of the Dossier — any number, including none — and place them face‑up in front of you. For each one you deploy, recover 1 exhausted die, or mend 1 Health if nothing is exhausted.
  2. Answer the evidence. Spend Research Pool cards on the Exhibits you just deployed (below). Exhibits deployed in an earlier session can no longer be answered.
  3. Strategy, twice. Commit dice, roll, and apply hits to your standing Exhibits: a hit is 1 success, a Crit is 2, and only the allowed colours may be applied. Then take up any dice you have not applied, add more if you want, and throw again. Nothing is compulsory.
  4. Resolve. Every Exhibit still standing deals its Damage to you. Then exhaust every committed die that applied nothing, and every die lying on an Exhibit that has just fallen. Recover up to REC exhausted dice, advance the attention, and tick the clock.

An Exhibit falls when the successes on it reach its Target. Dice applied to an Exhibit that is still standing stay on it, and the successes they scored stay with them — progress on an Exhibit is never lost, and those dice are neither available nor exhausted until the card falls.

What the Council lets slip

Five Council Tests print a Reward, and it still pays: read “if you succeed” as when this Exhibit falls, and take it during the Resolve step, after the dice on it have come back. The other three Reward cards modify a single attempt, which this scenario no longer has.

  • The Chamber Mechanism — recover 2 exhausted dice.
  • The Sedative Ledger — recover 3 exhausted dice.
  • The Vote — recover all exhausted dice of one colour.
  • Tracks in the Yard — recover 1 exhausted die of each of two colours.
  • The Narrow Stair — the next Exhibit you deploy has its Target reduced by 1.

These are the only reason it matters which Exhibit you finish first. Four of the five are Target 3 as dealt, so the cheapest evidence is the evidence that pays.

Answering an Exhibit

A retained card answers only its own domain, and reduces the Target of one Exhibit you have just deployed. A card you spend is gone. Candidates answer nothing at all.

Nine retained cards against fifteen Exhibits: the campaign answers a little over half the Dossier at best, and only while the right domains come up. No Exhibit drops below Target 1.

The decision the finale is built on

Deploying more than you must is how you get ahead of the clock, and each Exhibit pays you a die for it. Deploying more than you can bury is how you bleed. Count the Exhibits left against the sessions left and do not fall behind that number — slowing down when Health is short feels prudent and loses games, because the clock kills more reliably than the Council does.

Terminology: the Research Pool is the 18 carried‑forward cards. The Dossier is the 15 Exhibits. The dice pool is the Imposter’s dice. Card text that discards from the Research Pool never discards a die.

Standalone Imposter mode

You can play the finale without the campaign. Randomly select 3 eligible retained cards from each game and 3 more from each as Candidates, randomizing Stable versus Overheated independently for every retained AoS Advanced Program and keeping BAT to at most 1 Specimen. Then build the 15‑card Dossier normally and play the nine‑session finale.
