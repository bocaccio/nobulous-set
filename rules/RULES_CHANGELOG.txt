THE NOBULOUS SET — RULEBOOK AUDIT
Rulebooks checked line by line against Compendium v22, the printed card decks
(BAT v0.6, NAP v0.9c, AoS v10, NRJ v0.24) and the calibrated simulators
(bat.py, nap.py, aos.py, finale.py).  Everything below was MISSING or WRONG in
the previous PDFs and is now printed.


BAT — Build-a-Tyrant
--------------------
1.  THE TYRANT BATTLE HAD NO DICE PROCEDURE AT ALL.  The old text said who gets
    attacked and that Upgrades absorb damage, and stopped.  It never said where
    the Rats' dice come from, how many they roll, how the Tyrant decides how
    many dice IT rolls, or how the fight ends.  Now printed in full:
      - Battle pool: every personal die still in the game, from all four Rats,
        available and exhausted alike, swept into one shared pool, all
        available.  Banished dice stay gone.
      - Rats commit UP TO 6 DICE per round and REROLL EACH MISS ONCE (STR 2).
      - After the round, dice equal to the attacking Rat's REC + 1 return to the
        pool from the ones just committed.  The rest are exhausted for good.
        Nothing else refills the pool — the battle is a countdown, which is why
        it runs about five rounds.
      - The Tyrant rolls a number of its six dice equal to the HIGHEST VALUE
        among the Splices it still holds; 2 if it holds none.  Its dice never
        exhaust; it rolls fresh every round.
      - Damage order into the Tyrant: Defense Splices, remaining Splices, Buff
        HP, Health.  Each Splice soaks up to its Value, then is destroyed, and
        the overflow carries on.
      - Round order: untap, choose attacker, commit and roll, optionally tap one
        Upgrade, apply damage, recover, then the Tyrant's attack.
      - A Rat at 0 Health is knocked out, discards its Upgrades and cannot be
        chosen again; its Upgrades become campaign candidates.
2.  "UPGRADE VALUE" was used six times and never defined.  New Upgrades section:
    category, Value, tap effect; Value is the tie-break during the build and the
    damage the card absorbs during the battle; the 3-per-Rat limit includes
    Specimens; the Tyrant never taps a Defense Upgrade for an attack.
3.  Tyrant "REC 3" removed.  It was inherited from the old Assembly card and is
    meaningless — the Tyrant's dice do not exhaust.

3a. THE TYRANT'S DICE WERE NEVER RANDOM, AND ITS POOL WAS TWO THIRDS FICTION.
    The simulator committed the Tyrant's dice strongest-first out of a list
    written P P B B G Y.  The count is the highest Value among the Splices it
    still holds; Upgrade Values stop at 4, and 78% of attacks come after the
    biggest Splices are destroyed, so the count is 2 most of the time.  Measured
    over 19,203 attacks the old rule rolled Purple 89.8%, Blue 10.2%, Green
    0.0%, Yellow 0.0%.  A "fixed printed order" fix was tested and still left
    Yellow at 0.4%.  The Tyrant now DRAWS ITS DICE BLIND from a bag or cup,
    which is the only rule under which all four colors appear (33/33/17/17).
    It costs the Tyrant 18% of its damage per attack, so the Buff HP ladder is
    re-pegged: Easy 2, Standard 4, Hard 6, Brutal 8, Nobulous 8+2, for
    86/76/67/58/52 percent.  BAT ships 2 Buff HP cards; only Nobulous uses the
    second.  A Tier III campaign re-run confirms the finale is unaffected
    (71.5% -> 72.3%).

3b. THE TYRANT'S SPLICES WERE PURE ARMOR.  Every Upgrade prints a tap effect,
    and the Tyrant never used one - its five Splices were a wall of numbers.
    Now the FRONTMOST Splice applies its effect on every Tyrant attack: free,
    automatic, never tapped, and read off the card's PRINTED Value so damage
    already marked on it does not weaken the effect.  Killing that Splice is
    how you switch it off.  Cost to the Rats: 4-10 points, growing with
    difficulty.  Combined with blind dice draws (+8) it cancels almost exactly,
    so the ORIGINAL Buff HP table 0/2/4/6/8 is restored and reads
    87/77/66/60/50 percent.
    Rejected on measurement: a 'tap' version where using the effect stops that
    Splice absorbing (the Tyrant has no reason not to tap every round, so its
    front Splice becomes indestructible - 15% win rate at the top rung), and a
    'skip Defense Splices' version (identical numbers, extra exception).

3c. BUFF HP IS A 12-SPACE TRACK ON ONE CARD.  The Health die walks the Splice
    row, sitting on whichever card is being damaged and showing how much it has
    taken; when the row is spent the die moves onto the track and counts down,
    then onto Health.  One die runs the whole Tyrant, so the Tyrant side needs
    no other markers (measured: at most 1 partially-damaged Splice, ever).
3d. RAT UPGRADES DO NOT ACCUMULATE DAMAGE.  A Tyrant attack either meets an
    Upgrade's Value and destroys it - the excess carrying on to the next Upgrade
    under the same test - or it cannot meet any remaining Upgrade and stops
    there, reaching no Health.  No Rat card is ever part-damaged.  Only the
    Tyrant's Splices accumulate, and the Health die walking its row tracks them.
    Across 60,000 battles the worst case anywhere on the table is ONE
    part-damaged Splice and ZERO Rat Upgrades: the whole game runs on the 20
    Strong dice, the cards and the Chip, with nothing else on the table.
    The ladder re-pegs to Buff 0/3/6/9/12 for 89/79/66/56/45 percent.

3e. BUFF HP MOVED TO THE FRONT OF THE TYRANT'S ROW.  Damage order is now Buff
    HP, Defense Splices, remaining Splices, Health, and the Health die starts on
    the track at setup and only ever moves right.  This is a difficulty change,
    not a re-ordering: while you carve the Buff off, the Splices shelter behind
    it, so the Tyrant's frontmost-Splice effect and its dice count both stay at
    full strength.  Measured at Buff 9, the Tyrant commits 2.49 dice an attack
    against 2.18 with Buff HP at the back, and the shift is worth -4.7 points at
    Standard rising to -19.3 at the old Buff 12.  Each point of Buff HP is now
    worth about twice what it was, so the ladder returns to 0/2/4/6/8 for
    89/79/66/54/45 percent.  Tier III campaign 71.9% at Standard.

3f. THE SIMULATOR'S UPGRADE DECK WAS INVENTED.  Regular Upgrades were generated
    as random Values 2-3 and Specimens as random 3-4.  The printed deck is fixed
    and EVERY SPECIMEN IS VALUE 5; regular Values run 2,3,3,4 / 2,2,3,3 /
    2,3,3,4 / 2,3,2,4 by category.  'Defense' was also being guessed from an
    even Value rather than read from the card.  Every BAT number before this was
    measured against a deck that does not exist.

3g. THE SPLICE ROW IS SHUFFLED, and DIFFICULTY IS ONE NUMBER.  Sorting the row
    (Defense first, by type, by type then Value) all measured within 2 points of
    a full shuffle, so the row is randomized.  Sweeping the whole Specimens x
    Buff HP grid against the 84/78/70/60/51 target ladder found that setting
    both levers to the SAME number hits every rung within 2.4 points:
        Easy 2/2 84%, Standard 3/3 76%, Hard 4/4 69%, Brutal 5/5 61%,
        Nobulous 6/6 53%, and it keeps going - 7/7 is about 47%.
    Tier III campaign 71.8% at Standard, back on the 8/9/10 finale calibration.

3h. SPLICES ARE A FACE-UP STACK DURING PLAY, SHUFFLED INTO A ROW AT THE BATTLE.
    Setup leaves room for the Splice stack beside the Tyrant Attack deck; each
    Splice is added to it.  At the battle, lay the Buff HP card with the Health
    die on the difficulty number, shuffle the five Splices into a row, then set
    the Health dial to 20 at the right-hand end.
    Order is a presentation choice, not a balance one: splice order, category
    order, Value order and a full shuffle all sit within about a point.  But the
    Upgrade deck is richest at the start, so Splices arrive big first - the
    first Spliced averages Value 3.89 and the fifth 2.94.  Left in that order
    every fight peaks in round one and tapers.  Shuffled, it does not.

3i. RESOLVED — RED PLAYS IN THE BATTLE, AS A TRADE.  Red was never in the
    battle pool because the simulator stores it as a flag on whoever holds it
    rather than as one of that Rat's dice.  The rule adopted: Red is ALWAYS
    available to the attacking Rat and committed on top of the six pool dice,
    free and without exhausting - but only if that Rat does NOT tap an Upgrade
    that round.  Worth +2 to +2.8 points.
    A tap is usually more raw damage (Value-1, so 1 to 4) than Red's 7/6, so Red
    is the weapon of a Rat with nothing left worth tapping.  Playing the choice
    purely for damage puts Red on 5-11% of rounds; playing it to keep armor
    untapped puts it on 30-49% - and the two land WITHIN ONE POINT of each other
    at every rung, so the rule cannot be misplayed into a bad outcome.
    Rejected on measurement:
      - freely available every round (worth +6 to +12; moves the whole ladder)
      - every other round, as in the Splice Phase (55% of rounds, +6 to +12)
      - pinned to the leftmost undamaged Splice until it dies: the row dies fast,
        so Red returns on 77% of rounds - MORE generous than a cooldown
      - pinned to the rightmost undamaged Splice: 74% falling to 57%, never
        actually scarce, and an extra sentence to explain
      - folded into the shared pool as an ordinary die: only +2, and it loses
        the property that makes Red interesting - not costing a pool die
    Ladder returns to consecutive numbers: Easy 2, Standard 3, Hard 4, Brutal 5,
    Nobulous 6, for 85/78/70/63/55 percent, extending to 7 (~49%) and 8 (~42%).

3k. RED IS SET ASIDE, NOT EXHAUSTED - now stated outright.  The simulator has
    always treated battle Red this way (it is never added to the committed dice,
    so it never enters the recovery slice), but the rulebook said only that it
    was committed "for free".  Measured alternative, rejected: Red exhausting
    and being recovered through the normal REC step - which in practice costs
    one pool die per use, since a player always recovers the best die.  That is
    worth -0.6 to a damage-first player but -4 to -6 to one protecting armor,
    and it opens a 3 to 6 point gap between the two play styles where the
    set-aside rule keeps them within one point.  Red already pays by costing you
    the tap; charging twice turns the die that cannot miss into the die you
    should not touch.

3l. THE LADDER IS NOW PEGGED TO THE PARENT GAME.  The 20 Strong community stats
    sheet - 952 logged games - reports Solar Sentinels 49%, Too Many Bones 54%,
    Hoplomachus: Victorum 53%, and 53% across every deck.  20 Strong is designed
    as a coin flip.  BAT's Standard had been sitting at 78%.
        Easy 4 ~70%, Standard 5 ~63%, Hard 6 ~56%, Brutal 7 ~49%, Nobulous 8 ~42%
    Standard sits deliberately above the parent game's 53%: the simulator plays
    near-optimally every turn and never misreads a card, so a human's first-play
    rate will be lower than these figures.  The gap between the two is the one
    number simulation cannot supply - table reports should close it.
    Eight Specimens is the ceiling, so past Nobulous only Buff HP rises; the
    track runs to 12, where the Tyrant wins about three games in four.
    The NRJ tiers interleave between the rungs (Tier II is worth -6 to -7), so
    the combined surface offers about fifteen settings from 70% down to 36%.

3j. STAT DICE ASSIGNED.  Strategy die marks the active Lab Rat in both phases.
    Health die runs the Tyrant track.  The Recovery die is not used.


NAP — Nobulous Apprenticeship Program
------------------------------------
4.  FINF WAS NEVER EXPLAINED.  Setup said "set the FINF track to -1" and the
    card was never mentioned again.  Full section added: choose a setting before
    play; +1/+2/+3 shuffles FINF into the deck and adds Assignments to the next
    one, two or three Time Slots (ignore their Skulls, never exceed 4 in the
    row); -1/-2/-3 is that many uses per game, each removing a non-Final-Exam
    Assignment from the current row and exhausting its stored dice; not used in
    the Final Exam.
5.  HOW THE APPRENTICESHIP ENDS was missing.  It ends at 6 Credits OR when the
    Assignment deck runs out — both lead to the Final Exam.
6.  THE LOSS CONDITION was missing.  Health 0 = immediate loss, apprenticeship
    or exams.
7.  The unselected revealed Assignment is DISCARDED, and if the row already
    holds 4 both revealed cards are discarded and nothing is taken.
8.  The row PERSISTS across Time Slots.  The old text implied a fresh row.
9.  DEMAND TIMING was wrong.  Correct: at the end of a Time Slot, unresolved
    Assignments flip to Demand at no cost; an Assignment ALREADY on Demand and
    still unresolved inflicts its printed penalty, and again every slot it
    survives.
10. The color-matching rule governs STORING PROGRESS as well as completing.
11. Required Effort is waived when no legal commitment is possible.
12. A Credit Task completed on its DEMAND side still earns its Credit.
13. Opportunity effects reaching into the next Time Slot override restrictions
    printed on the Task cards they apply to.

    OPEN QUESTION FOR YOU — Slacker's passive reads "the first time each Day you
    VOLUNTARILY DRAW AN ADDITIONAL ASSIGNMENT".  No such action exists in the
    compendium, on the reference sheet, or in the simulator, so I have not
    invented one.  Either the rule was dropped (and Slacker's passive needs
    rewording) or it should read something like "you may take BOTH revealed
    Assignments if the row has space".  The second reading also explains the
    end-of-day discard's stated purpose.
    MEASURED, so the option is costed if you reinstate it: taking the second
    revealed Assignment is a trap, not a gift.  Used greedily it costs 20+ points
    (base game 32.4% -> 11.6%); used sparingly - only into a nearly empty row,
    only for a Credit Task in a subject you still need - it still costs 6 to 12
    (32.4% -> 21.0%, Slacker 48.7% -> 42.6%).  The row caps at 4, STR caps
    completions at 2 a slot, and every unresolved card flips to Demand and bleeds
    you.  Reinstating the rule would not be a giveaway; Slacker's Skull-ignore
    would be a small discount on a bad deal.


AoS — Automaton of Shale
------------------------
14. "RECOVER DICE EQUAL TO YOUR REC AT THE END OF EVERY PROGRAM" was missing
    entirely.  Without it REC 3 does nothing outside Advanced Programming and
    the game is unplayable as written.
15. DICE RETURN.  The old text listed the exhaustion penalties without ever
    saying the base case: when an element resolves its dice go BACK to the
    available pool, except the ones the gradient burns.  Allocation is not
    spending.
16. THE EXTRA STR ROLL is a FULL REROLL of one element's dice, replacing the
    previous result — better or worse.  Previously described only as "an extra
    roll".
17. PROGRAM REWARDS were printed on all 24 Program cards and never explained.
    Now: claim the printed reward on completing a Program with NO failed
    elements; Advanced Programs have none.  The six rewards are listed.
18. DECK CYCLING defined: a cycle is one shuffled pass through the remaining
    deck; set-aside and reward-removed Programs leave it permanently; the
    endgame starts after the third cycle or when the deck empties.
19. LOSS CONDITION added (Health 0), and the Health cap of 6.
20. An element may be given ZERO dice; it then simply fails.
21. Crit = 2 successes stated (it was only in the BAT book).


NRJ — Nobulous Research Journal
-------------------------------
22. TIER I NAP said "replace the 3 base Setbacks with the 4 Following
    Setbacks".  The calibrated configuration is 3 OF THE 4, chosen at random,
    in place of the 3 base Setbacks — one Following Setback sits out each game,
    mirroring how the base Setbacks work.  Fixed.  (The compendium says "the 4"
    in §6.2; the simulator's settled FOLLOW_MODE is replace3, and the -3/-12/-6
    tier deltas were measured with 3.)
23. The Imposter starts with ALL 17 DICE — never stated.
24. Finale loss condition added: Health 0 ends the finale; you replay the
    finale, not the campaign.
25. STR 2 in the finale spelled out as one roll plus one reroll of misses;
    Crit = 2.
26. Research Notes and Backdoor Routines: said where they live (face-up beside
    the table), that the claim condition and the one-shot use are both printed
    on the card, and that each can be claimed once.

VERIFIED CORRECT, NO CHANGE
    NAP Tier II Impostor Tasks do REPLACE a Credit Task of the same subject, and
    Tier III Devious Opportunities do REPLACE the six base Opportunities.  The
    compendium's looser "add" wording in §6.2 does not match the simulator; the
    rulebook already had it right.

2026-10-28 — BALANCE PASS, after the audit above
Two changes measured against the 47-57%% community band (955 recorded
games, mean 53%%).  Neither is a difficulty dial; both are printed on
the cards or in the books, so both need re-rendering.


AoS — GAINED A RULE
    On a Catastrophic Failure you may EXHAUST 2 MISSES ON FAILED
    ELEMENTS to negate the Health loss.  Count only misses still
    showing after any rerolls, and only on elements that actually
    failed.  Printed in aos.html under Catastrophic Failure.
    AoS read 35.0%% at Standard and 18.0%% at Nobulous, the lowest in
    the set, and was the one game the 2026-10-27 policy retune could
    not move — its policy was already the best available, so this was
    the game and not the player.  Standard at Tier III went 27.9%% to
    54.2%%.  The rule fires on 68%% of Catastrophic Failures.
    MISSES_TO_NEGATE in aos.py is the switch; 0 recovers the old rule.
    Still open: AoS reads 71.2%% played without NRJ against 54.2%% at
    Tier III, the widest solo-to-campaign gap in the set.


NAP — EVERY PRINTED TARGET ROSE BY 1
    The 26 Task REQUEST Targets, the 6 Opportunity Targets and the 12
    Final Exam BASE Targets.  Task DEMAND Targets did not move.
    NAP read above the band at every rung of its printed Credit ladder
    — Standard was 76.0%% played on its own — and the Credit goal could
    not fix both axes at any value, because the no-NRJ game leans on
    the Final Exam Targets and the goal does not touch them.  One more
    success on every check does: Standard is 49.8%% on its own and
    51.4%% at Tier III, all six Apprentices land in band on both axes,
    and the per-Apprentice spread NARROWS, 10.8 to 8.4.
    Raising the Demands as well was measured and rejected: it widens
    that spread to about 10 and drops three Apprentices out of band,
    whether all 26 rise or only the 14 described below.
    The printed ladder is unchanged — Easy 5 Credits, Standard 6,
    Hard 7, Nobulous 8 — but Standard has replaced Nobulous as the
    in-band rung, and Hard (37.8%%) and Nobulous (28.0%%) now sit below
    the band, in line with BAT's Nobulous at 28.1%%.

    WHAT THAT COST, and the wording that follows from it:
    14 of the 26 Tasks now print the SAME number on both faces, so on
    those cards flipping to Demand is no longer a Target rise — only
    the printed effect changes, and the penalty bites a slot later.
    The Request-to-Demand gap went from 1 x14, 2 x6, 4 x1, 5 x5 to
    0 x14, 1 x6, 3 x1, 4 x5.  nap.html said an unresolved Task flips
    to its Demand side, "higher Target", which is now false on 14 of
    26 cards; it reads "a harsher printed effect, and often a higher
    Target".  The simulator sees the Targets and the Demand penalties
    but not the per-side effects, so it cannot price what this does to
    how a flip FEELS.  That is a question for the table.

    One card's text followed the numbers: the NRJ Devious card BORROW
    THE MASTER'S NOTES named "printed Target 3 or less" on a
    Request-side non-Credit Task.  Those six Targets went 3,3,4,6,4,4
    to 4,4,5,7,5,5, so the card would have matched nothing.  Now "4 or
    less".  No other NRJ Target was raised — leaving the FOLLOWING,
    IMPOSTOR and DEVIOUS tables at their printed values is what keeps
    NAP's two axes converged.

RE-RENDERED THE SAME DAY
    All four rulebooks, all four print-and-play files and every card
    sheet were rebuilt on 2026-10-28 and copied out to images/,
    print-and-play/ and playtest/dist/assets/.  WeasyPrint's GTK
    libraries turned out to be present all along, in the conda tree,
    and only missing from PATH; see HANDOFF.md section 8.  BAT's and
    NRJ's rulebooks came back byte-identical, which is the check that
    the render environment is exact.

    That render also caught SIX SHEETS THAT WERE ALREADY STALE, none of
    them from this pass: the four AoS back grids, BAT_Rats_TTS_back and
    NAP_Apprentices_TTS_back.  The AoS backs had never been rendered
    since build_aos.py moved the flavour text to the foot of the card
    and put the Catastrophic Failure consequence in the footer.  They
    are correct now.

    NOW ON THE CARDS: the AoS footers were rewritten the same day, at
    Jon's direction, so a player reading only the card can see the
    escape.  They were also SHORTENED in the process — the old wording
    ran to a four-line block and the new one is three:

        1 failed element: this Program Overheats.
        2+ (Catastrophic Failure): lose 1 HP, or exhaust 2 misses on them.

    The Overheated face reads "is set aside" in place of the first
    clause.  Advanced Programs carry the whole thing in one sentence:

        2+ failed elements: lose 1 HP, or exhaust 2 misses on them;
        either way the next Advanced Program Overheats.  0-1: it stays
        Stable.  Recover between Advanced Programs.

    "EITHER WAY" IS LOAD-BEARING.  In aos.py the save sits inside
    `if failed >= 2:` and skips only `self.hp -= 1`, while
    `overheated = failed >= 2` is computed outside it.  A negated
    Catastrophic Failure is still a Catastrophic Failure: it still
    overheats the next Advanced Program, and on an ordinary Program the
    Overheated/set-aside consequence is driven by the failed element
    count and is untouched.  Wording that let the misses buy that back
    would print a rule the simulator does not play.
