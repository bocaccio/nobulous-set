2026-11-08  NEURAL RESPONSE SCREENING SPEAKS THE EXAM'S LANGUAGE
The Final Exam read "At least 1 Crit must contribute to complete this

Nothing else about the card changes: the subject, the Green dot and the base
Target of 6 are untouched.

AN EXAM HAS NOTHING TO COMPLETE.  rules/nap.html resolves one by reducing
its Target by your Credits in the subject, rolling against that Target while
honoring the card's printed restriction, and then LOSING 1 HEALTH FOR EACH
SUCCESS BELOW IT.  There is no completing an exam and no failing one; you
pass by surviving it.  So the clause was Assignment language on a card with
no Assignment step, and neither reading of it said anything a player could
act on.  Read as a commit rule it is unsatisfiable, because nobody commits FOR a
Crit - a Crit is a face you roll.  Read as a gate it dealt the card's whole
Target in 23% of the sittings it got, which is -3.9 points of win rate
across the solo column on one card of twelve, more than any other exam's
restriction costs.

THE EXAM IDIOM FOR CRITS ALREADY EXISTS, on two cards:
"A Red Crit counts as 1 success" (Final Imposter Examination) and "One
Yellow Crit counts as 3 successes" (Adaptive Reasoning Trial, the other
Engineering exam).  This card now joins them as the only one of the three
that applies to EVERY colour, and the flavour was already written for it:
electrodes on, try not to think anything incriminating.  A spike is exactly
what the screening flags, and a flagged response earns you nothing extra.

EVERY COLOUR, NOT ONE, AND THAT IS THE POINT.  A single-colour repricing on
this card would have been the same defect in a new coat: the commit policy
takes the strongest dice first, so a Green clause on a Green-dot exam would
often have found no Green die committed at all.  Measured over 9,914
sittings of this card in 36,000 games, a Crit contributes to 78% of them, so
the clause bites nearly every time the card is drawn, where the gate reading
bit 23% of the time and the unmodelled reading bit never.  The exam costs

2026-11-03  A RULES REVIEW, AND FOUR LEVERS THAT DID NOT SHIP
An outside read of all four rulebooks raised sixteen points.  Every one was
real.  Eleven were stale text with an authoritative answer already in the
repo, and are fixed: BAT's "set the dial to 20" against a difficulty table
of 9-14 (the dial is not built until Awakening); the Tyrant's six dice when
borrowing has banished the colour (start_battle recalls a banished die of
that colour straight into the bag); whether a breaking Splice may banish
dice you just committed (it may - splice_breaks runs before the Recover step
exhausts anything); NAP's "shuffle 3 of the 6 Setbacks" against a table of
4/4/5/6 (nap.LEVELS and RESULTS.txt both say the table); NAP recovering at
both ends of a slot (it recovers ONCE, at the top - time_slot recovers
before reveal and end_of_slot recovers nothing); NAP's objective implying
the Credit goal is the only road to the exam; NAP's blurb promising an exam
"built out of everything you neglected" when the pool is built from the
subjects you DID earn Credits in; AoS's blurb putting the Limitation after
the required-colour commitment; AoS's catastrophe mitigation reading as
redundant, which it is, on purpose; NRJ's reward timing, which session()
fixes as damage, exhaust, pay rewards, recover REC, advance; and NRJ's claim
that the five Rewards are the "only reason" finishing order matters, which
the same page contradicts twice.

DELIBERATE FAILURE IN AoS IS NOT A ROUTE, AND NEEDS NO NEW RULE.  The review
worked out that failing Programs shortens the deck, an empty deck advances
the game, and Advanced Programming is won by surviving rather than
completing - so dumping Programs looks efficient.  Measured, it is strictly
worse the more of it you do, down to 0.2% at Standard against 70.8% honest,
and 99.4% of those games are dead of Health before Advanced Programming is
reached at all.  The brake is the Program Reward: Health is only ever
regained by one, and a Reward needs zero failed elements, so failing on
purpose forfeits the only healing in the game.

Four levers were tried against it and NONE SHIPPED.  Capping the negate rule
per cycle costs honest play 14-18 points and the exploit nothing.  Retiring
the card on a Catastrophic Failure reads like a discount for dumpers but
actually deletes the Stable->Overheated escalation, so it flattens the ladder
AND makes dumping the best line at Standard.  A declared abandon priced in
Health is a dead button, because failing costs the same 1 Health with a ~68%
save attached.  Replaying the failed card at once is the best behaved of the
four and still wrong: it IMPROVES the dumping lines, because forcing a real
retry drags a dumper back to playing properly - it confiscates a choice
instead of punishing a strategy, and charges the honest player ~7 points.
All four are in simulation/aos.py, off, with their numbers beside them.

So the fix is the WORDING, which is what was actually broken.  "Complete all
three with Health remaining" never said whether the Advanced Programs have
to be completed; it is now "work through all three", with the survival rule
stated in Object of the game and a box saying plainly why failing on purpose
is not a shortcut.  No number moves.

THREE ORPHAN RULES REMOVED OR DEFINED.  Each was in the rulebook and nowhere
else - no card, no model, no lab - so none of them has ever been part of a
measurement:

  - NAP's "spend 1 STR to remove the initial Assignment" is deleted.  It
    never said which Assignment, when, or whether a mandatory Setback could
    go that way, and it duplicated FINF Reduce, which is the designed valve
    and is tied to its card's number.  (The loose any-card version of FINF
    Reduce was removed from the rulebook earlier for the same reason; see
    nap.FINF_REDUCE_ANY_CARD.)  If a STR-for-removal valve is wanted it is a
    new mechanic and wants simulating first.
  - AoS's Tucking is deleted.  As written set-aside Programs MAY be tucked
    under an Advanced Program and the exposed edge adds an element, which is
    pure added work for no return, so no player would ever choose it.  The
    intent was clearly that failures follow you into the finale, and the
    version that carries it is MANDATORY tucking - but that is an unmeasured
    difficulty increase on the endgame, so the dead text goes and the live
    option is recorded here rather than printed.
  - NAP's full row is defined rather than deleted.  A full row means no
    Primary is taken, and the Primary is what queues the next recovery
    colour, so nothing said what happens.  reveal() returns before
    `self.queued = primary.dot`, so the queued colour is UNCHANGED and the
    next slot recovers on the same colour again.  Printed.

STILL OPEN: NRJ states win rates in player-facing voice - 51% and 9% for a
full and empty Research Pool, "about 6 points" for a Research Note, "no more
than about four games in a hundred" for an Imposter ability - without the
simulation policy behind them.  They read as measured fact about play.

Rulebooks checked line by line against Compendium v22, the printed card decks
(BAT v0.6, NAP v0.9c, AoS v10, NRJ v0.24) and the calibrated simulators

BAT — Build-a-Tyrant
--------------------
1.  THE TYRANT BATTLE HAD NO DICE PROCEDURE AT ALL.  The old text said who gets
    attacked and that Upgrades absorb damage, and stopped.  It never said where
    the Rats' dice come from, how many they roll, how the Tyrant decides how
    many dice IT rolls, or how the fight ends.  Now printed in full:
      - Battle pool: every personal die still in the game, from all four Rats,
        available and exhausted alike, swept into one shared pool, all
        available.  Banished dice stay gone.
      - Rats commit UP TO 6 DICE per round and REROLL EACH MISS ONCE (STR 2).
      - After the round, dice equal to the attacking Rat's REC + 1 return to the
        pool from the ones just committed.  The rest are exhausted for good.
        Nothing else refills the pool — the battle is a countdown, which is why
        it runs about five rounds.
      - The Tyrant rolls a number of its six dice equal to the HIGHEST VALUE
        among the Splices it still holds; 2 if it holds none.  Its dice never
        exhaust; it rolls fresh every round.
      - Damage order into the Tyrant: Defense Splices, remaining Splices, Buff
        HP, Health.  Each Splice soaks up to its Value, then is destroyed, and
        the overflow carries on.
      - Round order: untap, choose attacker, commit and roll, optionally tap one
        Upgrade, apply damage, recover, then the Tyrant's attack.
      - A Rat at 0 Health is knocked out, discards its Upgrades and cannot be
        chosen again; its Upgrades become campaign candidates.
2.  "UPGRADE VALUE" was used six times and never defined.  New Upgrades section:
    category, Value, tap effect; Value is the tie-break during the build and the
    damage the card absorbs during the battle; the 3-per-Rat limit includes
    Specimens; the Tyrant never taps a Defense Upgrade for an attack.
3.  Tyrant "REC 3" removed.  It was inherited from the old Assembly card and is
    meaningless — the Tyrant's dice do not exhaust.

3a. THE TYRANT'S DICE WERE NEVER RANDOM, AND ITS POOL WAS TWO THIRDS FICTION.
    The simulator committed the Tyrant's dice strongest-first out of a list
    written P P B B G Y.  The count is the highest Value among the Splices it
    still holds; Upgrade Values stop at 4, and 78% of attacks come after the
    biggest Splices are destroyed, so the count is 2 most of the time.  Measured
    over 19,203 attacks the old rule rolled Purple 89.8%, Blue 10.2%, Green
    0.0%, Yellow 0.0%.  A "fixed printed order" fix was tested and still left
    Yellow at 0.4%.  The Tyrant now DRAWS ITS DICE BLIND from a bag or cup,
    which is the only rule under which all four colors appear (33/33/17/17).
    It costs the Tyrant 18% of its damage per attack, so the Buff HP ladder is
    re-pegged: Easy 2, Standard 4, Hard 6, Brutal 8, Nobulous 8+2, for
    86/76/67/58/52 percent.  BAT ships 2 Buff HP cards; only Nobulous uses the
    second.  A Tier III campaign re-run confirms the finale is unaffected
    (71.5% -> 72.3%).

3b. THE TYRANT'S SPLICES WERE PURE ARMOR.  Every Upgrade prints a tap effect,
    and the Tyrant never used one - its five Splices were a wall of numbers.
    Now the FRONTMOST Splice applies its effect on every Tyrant attack: free,
    automatic, never tapped, and read off the card's PRINTED Value so damage
    already marked on it does not weaken the effect.  Killing that Splice is
    how you switch it off.  Cost to the Rats: 4-10 points, growing with
    difficulty.  Combined with blind dice draws (+8) it cancels almost exactly,
    so the ORIGINAL Buff HP table 0/2/4/6/8 is restored and reads
    87/77/66/60/50 percent.
    Rejected on measurement: a 'tap' version where using the effect stops that
    Splice absorbing (the Tyrant has no reason not to tap every round, so its
    front Splice becomes indestructible - 15% win rate at the top rung), and a
    'skip Defense Splices' version (identical numbers, extra exception).

3c. BUFF HP IS A 12-SPACE TRACK ON ONE CARD.  The Health die walks the Splice
    row, sitting on whichever card is being damaged and showing how much it has
    taken; when the row is spent the die moves onto the track and counts down,
    then onto Health.  One die runs the whole Tyrant, so the Tyrant side needs
    no other markers (measured: at most 1 partially-damaged Splice, ever).
3d. RAT UPGRADES DO NOT ACCUMULATE DAMAGE.  A Tyrant attack either meets an
    Upgrade's Value and destroys it - the excess carrying on to the next Upgrade
    under the same test - or it cannot meet any remaining Upgrade and stops
    there, reaching no Health.  No Rat card is ever part-damaged.  Only the
    Tyrant's Splices accumulate, and the Health die walking its row tracks them.
    Across 60,000 battles the worst case anywhere on the table is ONE
    part-damaged Splice and ZERO Rat Upgrades: the whole game runs on the 20
    Strong dice, the cards and the Chip, with nothing else on the table.
    The ladder re-pegs to Buff 0/3/6/9/12 for 89/79/66/56/45 percent.

3e. BUFF HP MOVED TO THE FRONT OF THE TYRANT'S ROW.  Damage order is now Buff
    HP, Defense Splices, remaining Splices, Health, and the Health die starts on
    the track at setup and only ever moves right.  This is a difficulty change,
    not a re-ordering: while you carve the Buff off, the Splices shelter behind
    it, so the Tyrant's frontmost-Splice effect and its dice count both stay at
    full strength.  Measured at Buff 9, the Tyrant commits 2.49 dice an attack
    against 2.18 with Buff HP at the back, and the shift is worth -4.7 points at
    Standard rising to -19.3 at the old Buff 12.  Each point of Buff HP is now
    worth about twice what it was, so the ladder returns to 0/2/4/6/8 for
    89/79/66/54/45 percent.  Tier III campaign 71.9% at Standard.

3f. THE SIMULATOR'S UPGRADE DECK WAS INVENTED.  Regular Upgrades were generated
    as random Values 2-3 and Specimens as random 3-4.  The printed deck is fixed
    and EVERY SPECIMEN IS VALUE 5; regular Values run 2,3,3,4 / 2,2,3,3 /
    2,3,3,4 / 2,3,2,4 by category.  'Defense' was also being guessed from an
    even Value rather than read from the card.  Every BAT number before this was
    measured against a deck that does not exist.

3g. THE SPLICE ROW IS SHUFFLED, and DIFFICULTY IS ONE NUMBER.  Sorting the row
    (Defense first, by type, by type then Value) all measured within 2 points of
    a full shuffle, so the row is randomized.  Sweeping the whole Specimens x
    Buff HP grid against the 84/78/70/60/51 target ladder found that setting
    both levers to the SAME number hits every rung within 2.4 points:
        Easy 2/2 84%, Standard 3/3 76%, Hard 4/4 69%, Brutal 5/5 61%,
        Nobulous 6/6 53%, and it keeps going - 7/7 is about 47%.
    Tier III campaign 71.8% at Standard, back on the 8/9/10 finale calibration.

3h. SPLICES ARE A FACE-UP STACK DURING PLAY, SHUFFLED INTO A ROW AT THE BATTLE.
    Setup leaves room for the Splice stack beside the Tyrant Attack deck; each
    Splice is added to it.  At the battle, lay the Buff HP card with the Health
    die on the difficulty number, shuffle the five Splices into a row, then set
    the Health dial to 20 at the right-hand end.
    Order is a presentation choice, not a balance one: splice order, category
    order, Value order and a full shuffle all sit within about a point.  But the
    Upgrade deck is richest at the start, so Splices arrive big first - the
    first Spliced averages Value 3.89 and the fifth 2.94.  Left in that order
    every fight peaks in round one and tapers.  Shuffled, it does not.

3i. RESOLVED — RED PLAYS IN THE BATTLE, AS A TRADE.  Red was never in the
    battle pool because the simulator stores it as a flag on whoever holds it
    rather than as one of that Rat's dice.  The rule adopted: Red is ALWAYS
    available to the attacking Rat and committed on top of the six pool dice,
    free and without exhausting - but only if that Rat does NOT tap an Upgrade
    that round.  Worth +2 to +2.8 points.
    A tap is usually more raw damage (Value-1, so 1 to 4) than Red's 7/6, so Red
    is the weapon of a Rat with nothing left worth tapping.  Playing the choice
    purely for damage puts Red on 5-11% of rounds; playing it to keep armor

RETAINED OR NOT.  The spent/unspent split is gone.  A card's value depended on

THE DIFFICULTY LADDER, and it is cut with dice rather than with a dial.  The
Imposter begins with named dice exhausted; they recover normally, so the
handicap costs tempo rather than dice and bites hardest early.

  level      yellow  green  blue  purple   n    win    aim
  Easy          -      -      -      -     0   50.6%   +0.6
  Standard      3      2      2      2     9   39.2%   -0.8
  Hard          3      3      3      2    11   30.6%   +0.6
  Nobulous      3      3      4      4    14   19.4%   -0.6

The single red is never exhausted: session 5 allows only Red and Yellow and
there is one red die.

TWO THINGS HAD TO BE GOT RIGHT HERE.  First, the dice are NAMED rather than
chosen.  An earlier version let the player pick and the model picked badly -
all four colours come four to a pool, so "spend the commonest" broke the tie
on purple, the best die in the set, and the Nobulous rung measured WORSE
chosen (16.5%) than random (19.0%).  Modelling a free choice badly understates
every rung, and naming the dice removes the question.

Second, the counts are SPREAD across the colours instead of taking whole ones.
Whole colours collide with the attention wheel, which opens on Yellow with a
Yellow+Green window:

  4 yellow, 4 green                28.7%     same eight dice; the difference
  4 purple, 4 blue                 38.4%     is the wheel, not the handicap

Taken weakest-first the curve has a 14.6-point cliff between 6 and 8 dice,
exactly where the opening window hits zero, and a rung on a cliff is not a
setting.  Spread, the largest step in the whole curve is 6.1 points and the
ladder and the wheel stay independent - which matters, because the wheel's
phase is already chosen for where sessions 5 and 9 land.

AND THE RUNGS ARE STILL GAMES OF SKILL, which is the property that chose this
dial over REC or Dossier size.  The recovery decision - the one the attention
wheel exists to create - pays MORE as the ladder gets harder:

  Easy      pace +19.1   recover  +4.8   commit +12.3
  Standard  pace +20.4   recover +10.9   commit +19.8
  Hard      pace +16.7   recover +14.1   commit +14.8
  Nobulous  pace  +9.7   recover +10.6   commit  +7.7

THE PITCH IS 50.5%, and it is the best of the 224-cell policy grid: clock /
horizon / adaptive / small, the cell the module models.  REC 3 is reconfirmed
- the commit gap reads +23.8 / +12.3 / -12.0 / -13.2 at REC 2 / 3 / 4 / 5, so
3 is the last rung where holding dice back is correct at all.

IMPOSTER TASKS GET THE DOT EVERY ASSIGNMENT NEEDS, 2026-10-31
--------------------------------------------------------------
The 4 Imposter Tasks printed no recovery-colour dot.  Three printed NAP rules
read "that Assignment's color dot" - Required Effort, completing or storing
progress, and the colour the Primary queues for the next Time Slot - so on
these four cards all three references dangled and Red-as-wild meant nothing.

Nothing agreed on what the dot was.  The card printed none; simulation/nap.py
gave each Imposter Task the dot of the Credit Task it replaced, a different
colour every game, so NAP's Tier II and III calibration was measured against
dots that were never on a card; and the lab fell back to `dot||"P"`.
check_app_sync could not catch it because there was no field to compare.

  Neural Extraction Practicum   Anatomy      Purple
  Load-Bearing Prosthetics      Engineering  Blue
  Emergency Specimen Handling   Procedure    Yellow
  Containment Drill             Theory       Green

One of each colour, which keeps the deck's balance, and three of the four
take their own subject's most common Credit Task dot; Procedure takes Yellow
because Purple is spoken for and Yellow is its other colour.

PRINTING IT IS FREE.  4000 games a cell x 4 seeds, all six Apprentices:

  tier none   mean 54.7 -> 54.7   spread 7.8 -> 7.8
  tier II     mean 39.5 -> 39.6   spread 7.1 -> 7.4
  tier III    mean 56.7 -> 56.7   spread 6.6 -> 6.6

So the correction costs nothing and NAP's pitch stands.  check_app_sync now
compares the four dots across the generator, the lab and the model.
