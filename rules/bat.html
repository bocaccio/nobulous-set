The Nobulous Set · Game One

Designed by Jonathan Weeks

Build‑a‑Tyrant (BAT)

Four scientists, one scrapped monster, and not enough of it to go around.

An earlier Tyrant failed because nobody could control it. Four Lab Rats are stripping the carcass for Splices and building a Prototyrant they can direct. Every Experiment grafts one Splice onto their machine and lets the old Tyrant reclaim the other — so the question is never how many you get, it is which. Then the old thing wakes up, and you find out whether you built enough.

Components

  • 4 double‑sided Lab Rat cards
  • 20 Experiment cards and 1 Wake‑Up card
  • 16 Splice cards (4 each of Power, Mobility, Defense, Senses)
  • 8 Specimen cards
  • 7 Tyrant Attack cards
  • From 20 Strong: the 17 unit dice — 4 Purple, 4 Blue, 4 Green, 4 Yellow, 1 Red
  • From 20 Strong: the 3 stat dice — HP, STR, REC — and the Chip

Setup

  1. Choose one side for each of the four Lab Rat cards. That side is fixed for the game. Place all four Rats in a row — every Rat is in play at once.
  2. Give each Rat one Purple, one Blue, one Green and one Yellow die. These are that Rat’s personal dice and stay at their own workbench.
  3. Place the Red die on a randomly selected Lab Rat. Red is communal and belongs to no Rat.
  4. Choose a difficulty. It sets the Specimens that go into the old Tyrant’s carcass and the rounds you will get in the battle.
    DifficultySpecimensRounds
    Easy512
    Standard710
    Hard79
    Nobulous88
  5. Build the old Tyrant’s Splice deck: shuffle that many random Specimens in with the 16 ordinary Splices, deal 13 at random face‑down, and return everything else to the box. That deck is the carcass, and everything either side ends up with comes out of it.
  6. Build the Experiment deck: shuffle the 20 Experiments and deal 8. Take the last two of those eight, shuffle the Wake‑Up card in with them, and put that packet of three underneath the first six. You will resolve six, seven or eight Experiments, and you will not know which.
  7. Leave room for the Prototyrant’s grafts, the old Tyrant’s discard, and the Tyrant Attack deck.

Object of the game

The game is split into the Lab Phase and the Tyrant Battle. In the lab you and the old Tyrant divide its own carcass between you, one Splice each per Experiment. What is left in the carcass when the Wake‑Up card turns is the wall you then have to break, and what you grafted is what you have to break it with. Destroy the old Tyrant’s last Splice before the rounds run out to win.

One carcass, two halves Thirteen Splices go in and thirteen come out: every one is either grafted onto your Prototyrant or reclaimed by the old Tyrant. So a good Experiment does two things at once — it arms you and it shortens the wall — and a bad one does both in reverse. Nothing is discarded and nothing is wasted.

The Lab Phase (an Experiment)

  1. Reveal the next Experiment card. If it is the Wake‑Up card the lab ends at once — go to Wake‑Up.
  2. Reveal 2 Splices from the old Tyrant’s deck. If it is empty, shuffle its discard to make a new deck.
  3. Work out the Target: the two Splices’ combined Value minus the Experiment’s printed number, never less than 1. Rich Splices are harder to win and a high-numbered Experiment is a better method.
  4. Choose which Rat is active and put the Recovery die on it, after seeing the Experiment and the two Splices. The same Rat may be chosen on consecutive turns.
  5. Place the set‑aside Red die on a non‑active Rat.
  6. Resolve any ability that triggers after selection but before dice are committed.
  7. Commit dice, roll, and resolve (below).
  8. Exhaust the committed personal dice, resolve Red and any borrowed die, then recover dice equal to that Rat’s REC.

Committing and rolling

The active Rat may commit any number of its own available dice, plus Red if it holds it, plus at most one borrowed die. Hits and Crits count toward the Target.

Success Roll successes greater than or equal to the Target and satisfy any printed restriction. You choose which of the two Splices your Prototyrant grafts. The old Tyrant reclaims the other.
Failure The old Tyrant chooses, and it takes the higher‑Value Splice. Your Prototyrant grafts the other one. If the two are equal in Value, you choose which it takes.

Either way both Splices leave the deck: the graft goes face‑up with your Prototyrant and stays there, and the reclaimed Splice goes to the old Tyrant’s discard, where it waits to be shuffled back in and revealed again. Success never means more grafts. It means a better one.

Zero‑dice Experiments

A Rat may be selected and commit nothing. The Experiment fails automatically and the old Tyrant takes the better Splice. Recovery still happens. This is a legal choice, not a rest action — it gives no bonus.

Splices

Every Splice — ordinary or Specimen — has a category (Power, Mobility, Defense or Senses), a Value, and a printed effect. Specimens are all Value 5. Which side holds it decides what its Value means:

  • In the lab, Value is the stake: the two revealed Values set the Target, and the higher one is what the old Tyrant takes when you fail.
  • Grafted on your Prototyrant it becomes a graft: its Value is the dice cap for the round it is exposed in, and the threshold an incoming attack must meet to destroy it. Its printed effect is yours to use.
  • Reclaimed by the old Tyrant it stays a Splice: its Value is the damage you must land in one blow to break it, and the number of dice it rolls back at you. A Splice’s printed effect is used only while it still stands.

There is no limit on grafts. Your Prototyrant will finish the lab holding six, seven or eight grafts.

Borrowing

The active Rat may borrow one available non‑Red die from another Rat, before either Strategy roll. The bigger your Prototyrant has grown, the more of the lab’s equipment it is already using, and the worse the die you are allowed to take:

0Purple, Blue, Green or Yellow17%
1Blue, Green or Yellow33%

3 or moreYellow only67%

After the Experiment resolves, roll the borrowed die by itself. On a miss it is banished — removed from the game permanently. On a hit or Crit it returns to its owner exhausted. Put a banished die on the card of the Rat it was borrowed from: that Rat is a die short for good, and no exhausted die of that color goes back to it.

The Red die

  • Red is communal, is never banished, and belongs to no workbench.
  • If the active Rat holds Red, it may commit it normally.
  • In the lab, after Red is used it is set aside rather than exhausted.
  • At the start of the next Experiment, after the active Rat has been chosen, place the set‑aside Red on any non‑active Rat. If Red was not used it is not set aside, and this step is skipped.

In the battle Red belongs to whichever Rat holds it and only the active Rat may commit it, as in the lab. Two things change. It counts toward the exposed graft’s dice cap, so taking it costs you a pooled die; and it exhausts like any other committed die, so it comes back only on a recovery. Hand it to a non‑active Rat at the end of each round either way.

Wake‑Up

The Wake‑Up card ends the lab the instant it is turned, wherever you are in the deck. Nothing about the split changes: what you grafted is yours and what the old Tyrant reclaimed is its wall.

  1. Combine the old Tyrant’s undrawn deck and its discard, shuffle them, and stand them up as its Splices — its wall, five to eight cards deep depending on how the lab went.
  2. Shuffle your grafts into the Prototyrant’s own face‑down pile.
  3. Start the Prototyrant’s Health at 12, on paper or any counter you like. All four Rats share it — damage nothing absorbs comes off this one number, and at 0 the Rats lose.

The Tyrant battle

Setting up the fight

  1. Shuffle the 7 Tyrant Attack cards face‑down and leave them that way. The Attack discard starts empty, so the Rats’ first attack is unrestricted.
  2. Take 2 Purple, 2 Blue, 1 Green and 1 Yellow out of the Rats’ personal dice — there are only 17 in the game, so the old Tyrant’s six come from your own supply. Keep them in a bag. Those six never exhaust; it draws from them fresh every round.
    If a color is short — borrowing banished too many of it — take dice of that color back off the Rats they were banished on. They are not given back; they go straight into the bag. If there is no die of that color anywhere, the Tyrant goes without and draws from a smaller bag.
  3. Sweep every personal die still in the game into one shared battle pool: those that were available stay available, those that were exhausted stay exhausted, and then recover 2. Banished dice stay gone. Red is not part of the pool — it stays with its holder, on the terms under The Red die.
  4. Lab Rat abilities do not apply in the battle — they are laboratory technique, and help only with Experiments.

A battle round

  1. Reveal two grafts from the Prototyrant’s pile and the top Splice of the old Tyrant’s wall. Choose one graft to use and expose; the other is set aside until the pile is next shuffled, where nothing can destroy it. When the pile runs out, shuffle the grafts that survive and carry on.
  2. Choose the active Rat from those still standing and move the Recovery die to it. Its printed ability is silent here.
  3. Commit dice from the shared pool, up to the exposed graft’s Value and honoring the commit color, and roll them. Every hit is 1 damage and every Crit is 2, and STR 2 gives a second Strategy roll here too. With no graft revealed at all, the cap is 3.
  4. Either use the exposed graft’s effect or commit Red — not both. Red counts toward the cap. Dice a graft’s effect explicitly adds may exceed it.
  5. Apply the damage. A Splice does not chip: your blow must meet its printed Value in this one attack or it bounces off and the whole attack is wasted. Nothing carries over to the next Splice. Meet it and the Splice is destroyed outright — and if it was the last one, the Rats win immediately.
  6. Retaliation. The old Tyrant attacks even if you just broke the Splice that was facing you (below).
  7. Count the round. If its last Splice is still standing when your difficulty’s rounds are used up, it finishes the job and the Rats lose.
The recovery pass Before committing dice the active Rat may pass: make no attack and forgo the exposed graft’s effect. The old Tyrant retaliates as normal and the graft defends as normal — it may well survive. Afterwards recover REC + 2 dice from the exhausted pool. You may pass with a graft revealed, and with the rounds counting down it is never free.
Committing by color Every Tyrant Attack card has a colored dot in the top‑right corner of its title band (3 Green, 3 Yellow and 1 Blue). That dot is the commit color. The one that counts is on the top card of the Attack discard, which is empty until the Tyrant has attacked — the Rats’ first attack is unrestricted. When a Rat attacks it must commit at least one die of that color. If no die of that color is available in the shared pool, it must instead commit two dice of one other color. Red never counts towards the commit color.

The old Tyrant’s retaliation

  1. Reveal the top Tyrant Attack card and apply it. Discard it face‑up afterwards; reshuffle the discard if the deck runs out. Its color dot is now the commit color for the next Rat attack.
  2. It draws dice equal to the exposed Splice’s Value — never more than its six — at random, without looking, rolls them, then returns them to the bag. A Splice you destroyed this round still sets that number, but its printed effect does not fire. A Splice that survived applies its effect as well.
  3. The exposed graft defends, whether or not you attacked with it. If the attack meets or exceeds its Value that graft is destroyed — discard it, and it is gone for the rest of the game. If it holds, it cycles back into the Prototyrant’s pile. Only damage with no graft to absorb it reaches the Prototyrant’s Health.

Destroy the old Tyrant’s last Splice to win. It wins if the Prototyrant’s Health reaches 0, or if its last Splice is still standing when the rounds run out; either way you replay BAT.

Armor

Two kinds of armor Neither kind chips. A Splice must be met in one blow or it takes nothing; a graft stops an attack dead whatever the blow was worth. The difference is the price: a Splice stands until a big enough hit lands, a graft is destroyed the moment a big enough attack finds it and never comes back. The old Tyrant’s armor is a wall. Yours is a stock, and it runs down.

Fine‑tuning

The round count is the dial. A round is worth roughly a tenth of your chances, so take one off for a harder game and add one for a kinder one — and if you want the fight itself to bite harder rather than be shorter, raise the Specimens instead: that makes each Splice dearer to break without adding another one to the wall. Start the Prototyrant above 12 for a kinder game; 15 is as high as it was built to go.

Carrying BAT forward

Only relevant if you are playing the Nobulous Research Journal (NRJ) campaign, which is Tier III only. Skip otherwise.

BAT contributes its 6 Research Notes, and nothing leaves the carcass. A Note you claimed is retained, up to 2; one still lying face‑up unclaimed is a candidate. It does not matter whether you went on to spend it. A retained Note answers Physical Exhibits in the finale, reducing a Target by 2. Claiming one costs you the choice: on your next completed Experiment the old Tyrant picks the Splice it reclaims, so the Rats build thinner for it — BAT is harder so the Imposter is better armed.
