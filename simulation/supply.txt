CAN THE CAMPAIGN'S CARDS BE SAVED? 1200 games per game per level

1. SUPPLY - printed cards against what the rules ask for

   NAP   3 Final Exams faced + 3 unused           12 printed, needs 6  ok
   AoS   3 Advanced Programs faced + 3 unused      8 printed, needs 6  ok
   BAT   up to 3 Upgrades held + Splices to fill 6 24 printed, needs 6  ok

   The Tyrant always carries exactly 5 Splices, so BAT's Candidate
   supply from the Tyrant alone is 5: 3 retained needs 3 of them,
   1 retained needs 5, and a win with NO retained Upgrade would
   need 6 and come up one short. That did not happen in any win
   measured below, but the rules permit it - winning the battle
   having completed no Experiments - and they used to be silent
   on it. rules/bat.html now fills from unused Upgrade cards when
   the Splices do not cover it, which is the source Splicing
   Machine Malfunction already uses to top the Tyrant up to 5.

2. BANKING - what each game leaves on the table when it is won

   BAT: Upgrades the Rats hold when the battle begins
                  win    mean  3+ held  Specimen    2 Spec
   Easy         63.7%    4.37      92%       87%       43%
   Standard     56.1%    4.38      94%       91%       56%
   Hard         44.1%    4.29      93%       92%       62%
   Nobulous     28.1%    4.36      95%       94%       70%

   So the three retained slots fill on nearly every win, and a
   Specimen - worth -5 against an Upgrade's -3 - is there to take
   almost always. The 'at most 1 Specimen' cap is the binding
   rule, not the supply: a second Specimen is on the table in the
   last column's share of wins and has to be left behind.

   AoS: Stable / Overheated of the 3 Advanced Programs faced
                  win  Overheat               commonest hand
   Easy         37.9%       34% Stable / Overheated / Overheated
   Standard     30.6%       35% Stable / Overheated / Overheated
   Hard         19.7%       38% Stable / Overheated / Overheated
   Nobulous     12.6%       36% Stable / Overheated / Overheated

   The first Advanced Program is Stable by rule, so a retained AoS
   hand can never be all-Overheated and the finale's flat 50/50
   was never reachable. Stable is -4 and cannot be discarded to
   avoid; Overheated is -2 and can - so the real hand reduces more
   and avoids less than the model assumed.

   NAP: nothing to measure. final_exam draws pool[:3] from 12
   printed exams, so exactly 3 are faced and retained in every
   game that reaches the exam, and 9 remain to draw 3 Candidates
   from. NAP's contribution is 3 retained + 3 Candidates always.

4. CARRY-FORWARD RULES - do the four documents agree?

   rules/bat.html   retains 'the ones they held when the battle
                    began... it does not matter whether the card
                    or its holder survived', with Tyrant Splices
                    as the Candidates
   rules/nrj.html   agrees - checked on this run
   rules/bat.html   self-consistent - checked on this run
   rules/nap.html   3 Final Exams faced + 3 unused = 6          ok
   rules/aos.html   3 Advanced Programs faced + 3 unused = 6    ok

   Two copies of the old rule were stale when the audit ran and
   both have been corrected. nrj.html's Research Pool table
   predated the bat.html rewrite; bat.html's own knockout step
   also still said a knocked-out Rat's Upgrades 'become
   candidates rather than retained cards', three paragraphs from
   the rule saying it does not matter whether the card or its
   holder survived - 'the Rats are not the imposters, they chose
   what went into one, and winning proves the choice was right.'

5. THE PLAYTEST TABLE - the other place the hand-off is entered

   playtest/dist/suite.js keeps the Research Pool as a COUNT of 18
   and six number inputs for what is being spent, because the
   player holds the physical cards. Three things came out of
   auditing it:

   fixed   drawNRJ guarded on `if(state.nrj.current||...)`, a
           falsy test on a card INDEX. Scenario index 0 is The
           Physician's Greeting, so with that card on the table
           pressing Reveal again silently discarded it, drew a
           replacement and counted no attempt. Verified in the
           running app before and after: current 0 -> 7 with the
           attempt count unmoved, now unchanged. Every other read
           of nrj.current already used === null.
   fixed   the six spend inputs had no maximum, so a slip could
           spend more cards of a kind than the pool holds. Caps
           are now 3 per retained kind, 1 for the Specimen, 9 for
           wrong-category.
   fixed   `eligibleAvoid` was a flat 3 and the pool-mode selector
           only changed the overlay label. These were one issue:
           the app could not derive the avoid count because it
           tracked the pool as a COUNT of 18 rather than as
           cards, and standalone mode offered a random sample
           without dealing one.

           Standalone mode now deals the 18 cards rules/nrj.html
           describes - 3 retained and 3 Candidates per game,
           retained AoS randomised Stable/Overheated
           independently, BAT held to at most 1 Specimen - and
           the player spends them by clicking. So the Target
           reduction is computed from the cards actually spent, a
           spent card is gone for the rest of the finale, the
           avoid count is read off the pool (3 to 5, as section 2
           predicts), and 'The Long Argument: spend at most one
           NAP card' is now enforceable and enforced. Avoiding
           discards a Candidate before a retained Overheated
           Program, since a Candidate reduces nothing anyway.

           Campaign mode keeps the manual tally - there the
           player holds the physical cards - and gains one setup
           input, how many retained Overheated Programs were
           banked, which is exactly what the avoid count needed.
   fixed   drawNRJ rebuilt the pool when the Scenario deck ran
           empty, which silently restarted the finale. Twelve
           Scenarios against nine attempts means it cannot
           happen, but losing a game in progress is a worse
           answer than doing nothing, so it now does nothing.
