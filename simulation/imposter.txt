3. THE TRADE - 'lose 1 Health OR banish 2 available dice'

   The choice is resolved between the commit and the recovery -
   rules/nrj.html steps 4, 5, 6 - which is the trough of the pool.

   available dice at that instant   0.11 (mean)
   none at all                      97.8% of failures
   able to pay in dice              2.1% of failures
   actually paid in dice            1.9% of failures
   banished over a whole game       0.16 dice of 17

   So the one clause that makes dice a currency is unavailable in
   the great majority of the failures that offer it. The printed
   rule even anticipates this - 'if you cannot banish 2 available
   dice, Health loss is mandatory' - so the finale is a Health
   race with a dice floor, not a dice-management puzzle. Same
   shape as BAT's Accelerated Trials: a cost billed after the
   spend finds an empty pool.

4. DICE CLAUSES - the Scenarios that restrict the dice themselves

   Six of the 24 Council Tests constrain which dice may
   contribute, and none was modelled until this audit.

                                     drawn  blocks it
   An Open Window                     1593        75%
   The Long Session                   1536        74%
   Order of Precedence                1500        73%
   An Old Injury                      1559        33%
   The Ceremonial Seal                1574        22%
   The Darkened Corridor              1502         0%

   all six modelled       61.7%
   all six switched off   63.5%

   Three of them block the majority of the attempts they appear
   in, and the whole set is worth -1.8 points. That is the
   finding, not a reassurance: by the time these clauses bite,
   the attempt was already lost, and being blocked SAVES the dice
   and the Research cards it would have wasted. There is nothing
   left to squeeze.

   The color clauses only have teeth when the pool is thin, and
   repair H gave two of the three some: they used to block 0% of
   their attempts and now block 39% and 27%, because a pool of
   two or three dice may simply not hold the Purple the card
   asks for. The third bars YELLOW, and a pool recovered
   strongest-first holds none, so it still blocks 0% - inert as
   printed. The other two are a tax on the recovery decision -
   recover the strongest dice and you keep the Purple but lose
   the spread; recover for color and you throw weaker dice.
   That is a decision the finale did not previously contain.

5. POLICY - four choices in nrj.py that are not printed rules

   recover the last dice appended             60.9%  (-0.8)
   banish the last die in the list            60.5%  (-1.2)
   withhold the trade below 8 dice            61.7%  (+0.0)
   commit nothing at an unreachable Target    59.4%  (-2.3)

   Recovering the worst dice rather than the best costs 1
   points on its own, and it was an accident of list order:
   commit() filled the exhausted pile strongest-first and REC
   popped the end of it. The two banish dials are worth -1.2 and
   +0.0, for the reason section 3 gives - the trade is already
   unpayable, so how it would be paid changes nothing. Committing
   at a Target the pool cannot reach was tested and KEPT:
   withholding the dice is worth -2.3, because 'expected
   successes below the Target' is not the same as impossible, and
   a long throw can still crit its way home.

6. DIALS - one step each

   REC 2            35.1%  (-26.6)   mean pool  4.3
   REC 4            78.9%  (+17.2)   mean pool  5.9
   3 Health          2.2%  (-59.5)   mean pool  5.7
   5 Health         61.7%  (+0.0)   mean pool  5.0
   STR 3            77.8%  (+16.1)   mean pool  5.2
   8 attempts       80.0%  (+18.2)   mean pool  5.4
   10 attempts      20.7%  (-41.0)   mean pool  4.8

   Every dial is a cliff. One point of REC is worth 17 to 27
   points of win rate and one point of Health 0 to 59, against
   roughly 4 for a point of Tyrant Health in BAT. Nine attempts
   each near the pass/fail line, with Health covering only three
   failures, is a binomial knife edge - the finale is fragile
   rather than tense, and it cannot be tuned with these dials
   because there is no setting between far too easy and far too
   hard.

7. RESEARCH POOL - the other budget

   Target reduction bought   18.3 over the finale
   cards left unspent        5.7 of 18
   of those, retained        0.0 (the rest reduce nothing)

   Not one retained card goes unspent: everything left in the
   pool at the end is a Candidate, which reduces nothing. The
   budget is spent out by the fourth attempt - see the Target
   rows in section 2, where the spent Target falls to 3.7 and is
   back at the printed value by attempt 7. The finale's whole
   reduction budget covers four attempts of nine.
