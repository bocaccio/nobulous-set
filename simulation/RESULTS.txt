  WHY THIS ONLY SURFACED NOW.  Two model defects had been
  hiding each other since the abilities were first modelled:

    Debugger    the model spent the extra STR roll whenever an
                element came up short, so Debug could only fire
                on the POST-reroll total.  That is wrong play,
                not a house rule - an extra roll is a full
                reroll that stands "better or worse" AND
                exhausts the element's dice regardless of
                outcome, while Debug was one die and free, so a
                player on Target-1 declines the roll and Debugs.
                Misplayed: 68.0%.  Played properly: 88.4%.
    Architect   Reconfigure prints "move UP TO 2" and the model
                had no way to decline, so under triage it
                stripped the buffer off elements that were
                going to pass to load the one it had given up.
                Misplayed: 32.4%.  No ability at all: 47.2%.
                Played properly: 54.3%.

  One card 20 points too strong and one 15 too weak very nearly
  cancel in a mean of six, which is exactly what the published
  AoS row was.  Fixing both widened the spread from 35.6 points
  to 41.3 - the right direction, because the spread was always
  there and the mean was hiding it.  Replacing the Debugger's
  ability then closed it to 7.5.

  THE FIX, APPLIED 2026-11-05: A DIFFERENT ABILITY, NOT A
  SMALLER ONE.  Debug now reads "Once per Program, before
  rolling an element, swap 1 of its dice for a die from your
  available pool."  One clause, 99 characters, three printed
  lines, and no cadence beyond once per Program.

  THE TRIGGER WAS THE PROBLEM, NOT THE FREQUENCY.  The old
  card read "Once per Program, after failing by exactly 1
  success, reroll 1 allocated die.  If it still fails, exhaust
  that die" - three clauses, the last of them a no-op, since a
  failed element already exhausts every die on it.  A reroll
  only pays when you are short by exactly 1, so its value
  climbs with the difficulty, and metering is then the only
  lever left.  Metering it DOES work - once per cycle and once
  in Advanced Programming reads 62.7% on a 25.5 slope - but it
  buys a second cadence clause and still leaves the card 8.4
  clear of the roster.  An ability whose payout does not depend
  on having missed needs no metering at all.

  The two worst slopes, dbg_reroll at 10.2 and link at 16.5,
  are the same disease from two directions: a reroll and a
  banked success are both worth most exactly when you are
  short.  dbg_cost is why the penalty clause was never the
  answer - the cost is one die and the ability buys Health,
  which is the only thing that can lose the game.  The swap is
  a colour upgrade worth about half a success every Program
  whatever the rung, so it inherits the floor's slope.

  SACRIFICE_SHORT BEATS HONEST IN ALL SIXTEEN CELLS, by +2.7 to
  +8.0, mean about +6.  sacrifice-always does not: it LOSES 1.5
  to 4.1 at Easy and wins 2.9 to 5.2 at the hard end, which is
  the shape the old paragraph described.  sacrifice_short has no
  such weakness, so it is a dominant line, and at Standard it
  puts AoS at 60.6% - ABOVE the 47-57 band, where honest reads
  52.7%.

  WHAT IT IS, EXACTLY.  Give the dearest element only the single
  die the allocation floor obliges you to give it, and fill every
  other element to its Target+1 cap - but ONLY when the pool
  cannot fill them all anyway (aos.py: len(pool) < sum(t+1)).
  That gate is what separates it from sacrifice-always, which
  does it even when the dice are there, and wastes the Program
  Reward for nothing.  It is legal as printed, it needs no memory,
  and the trigger is countable on the table, so a playtester will
  find it.

  WHY IT IS NOT THE SAME HOLE THE FLOOR CLOSED.  The floor took
  away the FREE version - a bare element exhausted no dice and
  cost no Health - and that was worth up to +27.  What is left
  is worth about +6 and is paid for: the victim still takes a
  die, and the Program Reward is still forfeit.  The residue is
  the value of CONCENTRATING dice, which is the decision the game
  is about, so it cannot be removed without removing the game.

  TWO WAYS TO SETTLE IT, both measured.  The choice was a design
  decision and it was Jon's; he took option 1 on 2026-11-05.

    1. CHOSEN.  Pitch against the better policy.  Every
       difficulty name moves one rung down - Easy 3/3/2,
       Standard 2/3/3, Hard 1/3/4, Nobulous 0/3/5 - and
       ALLOC_MODE ships as sacrifice_short.  0/3/5 is the printed
       construction rule's OWN next step ("Specialized stays at 3
       throughout; each step up trades one Routine Program for
       one Unstable"), so no rules SENTENCE changed - only the
       table in rules/aos.html, the labels in index.html, and
       aos.LEVELS.
    2. NOT CHOSEN.  Leave it, treating +6 for a policy a player
       has to notice, and which costs the Reward every time it
       fires, as a skill ceiling rather than an exploit, with the
       printed ladder honest about ordinary play.

  WHAT IT ACTUALLY COST: almost nothing.  Measured 2026-11-05 at
  20,000 games a cell, the harder ladder and the stronger policy
  very nearly cancel.

                      before            after
                  (honest, 3/3/2)  (sac_short, 2/3/3)
    Easy               63.1%             60.1%
    Standard           52.9%             51.7%

    Nobulous           36.0%             36.0%

  the published pitch survives and so does the calibration behind

  THE ONE THING TO WATCH.  Honest play on the new ladder reads

  MASS DUMPING IS NOT A ROUTE THROUGH AoS (2026-11-03)
  Raised in review: failing Programs shortens the deck, an empty
  deck advances the game, and Advanced Programming is won by
  SURVIVING three Programs rather than completing them - so failing
  on purpose might be efficient.  Measured with aos.ALLOC_MODE,
  6000 games a cell, the negate rule as shipped:

                       honest  dump Uns  dump S+U  dump all
    Easy  5/3/0         79.9%     79.9%     33.6%      0.3%
    Standard  4/3/1     70.8%     64.1%     18.9%      0.2%
    Hard  3/3/2         62.7%     43.8%      9.3%      0.2%
    Nobulous  2/3/3     52.6%     26.9%      4.4%      0.3%

    Strictly worse, monotonically in how much you dump.  (Easy has
    no Unstable Programs, so dumping them is honest play - which is
    why those two cells agree exactly.)  The premise never lands:
    99.4% of dump-everything games are dead of Health BEFORE
    Advanced Programming, against 21% of honest ones at Standard.

    The brake is NOT the negate rule.  It is that Health is only
    ever regained through a Program Reward, and a Reward requires
    ZERO failed elements - so failing on purpose forfeits the only
    healing in the game, from a start of 2 HP, needing ~16 failures
    to burn the deck down, with negation only a ~68% save.

    Capping the negate rule per cycle was considered and dropped:
    it costs honest play 13.7 / 16.6 / 17.2 / 18.3 points (cap 1)
    or 3.2 / 5.3 / 5.8 / 6.7 (cap 2) and costs the dumping line
    0.1 to 0.4, because there is no exploit there to tax.  Honest
    play wants the save ~3.2 times a game; a cap of 1 denies 39%.

  THREE MORE LEVERS, NONE ADOPTED (2026-11-03)
  Asked whether deliberate failure could be made a real option
  rather than a trap.  All three are in aos.py, off, with the
  numbers beside them.  Win rate, honest play, 4200 games a cell:

                        honest now   cap 1   CF->aside  abandon  retry
    Easy  5/3/0              79.2%   -13.7      +3.4      -6.1   -6.2
    Standard  4/3/1          70.8%   -16.6      +2.9      -8.3   -7.2
    Hard  3/3/2              62.8%   -17.2      +4.4      -8.9   -7.8
    Nobulous  2/3/3          52.1%   -18.3      +7.5      -8.3   -6.8

    CF_SETS_ASIDE (a Catastrophic Failure retires the card) does
    not discount dumping so much as delete the Stable->Overheated
    escalation: a bad failure stops making the card harder and
    starts making it vanish.  Honest play gains most at Nobulous,
    so the ladder FLATTENS, and dumping Unstable becomes the best
    line at Standard - 74.9% against 73.7% honest.

    ABANDON_MODE (declare it, pay Health, card leaves the deck) is
    a dead button at every price: abandoning costs 1 Health for
    certain where failing costs 1 Health with a ~68% save and might
    still come good.  Nobody would press it.

    RETRY_IMMEDIATE (replay the failed card at once, no dumping)
    is the best behaved - dumping stays dominated everywhere, the
    ladder keeps its shape, and it adds no table time.  But it
    IMPROVES the dumping lines by +2 to +11, because forcing a real
    retry drags a dumper back to playing properly.  It confiscates
    a choice rather than punishing a strategy, and the only player
    who pays is the honest one, about 7 points.

    CONCLUSION: nothing is wrong with the system - the economy
    already punishes deliberate failure by 50 to 80 points, because
    Health comes only from Program Rewards and a Reward needs zero
    failed elements.  What is wrong is the WORDING, which never
    says the three Advanced Programs must be completed.  Fix the
    sentence, not the system.

  above, BAT steps off a threshold cliff - NAP goes 42.1% to
  76.0% at Standard, the finale 50.9% to 61.6%, BAT 46.5% to
  53.2%. AoS does not move at all: Target + 1 is a rules cap, so
  it had no over-commit to tune. AoS's 35.0% is a statement
  about the game; the other three were statements about the
  player. Re-pitching the ladders is a design decision.

  TWO OF THOSE DECISIONS HAVE NOW BEEN MADE, both 2026-10-28, and
  neither is a difficulty dial. AoS gained a RULE: on a
  Catastrophic Failure you may exhaust 2 misses on failed
  Elements to negate the Health loss, which took Standard at
  Tier III from 28.7% to 53.5% and fires on 68% of Catastrophic
  Failures. NAP had every printed TARGET raised by 1 - the 26
  Task Requests, the 6 Opportunities and the 12 Final Exam bases
  - with the Task DEMANDS deliberately left alone. That scope is
  the only one measured that puts all six Apprentices in band on
  both axes. Raising the Demands as well widens the Apprentice
  spread from 7.9 to about 10 points and drops three of the six
  out of band, whether all 26 rise, or only the 14 whose
  Request-to-Demand gap the change collapsed to zero.

  WHAT THAT COST, recorded because the rulebook has to match it:
  14 of the 26 Tasks now print the SAME number on both faces, so
  on those cards flipping to Demand is no longer a Target rise -
  only the printed effect changes, and the penalty bites a slot
  later. The gap went from 1 x14, 2 x6, 4 x1, 5 x5 to 0 x14,
  1 x6, 3 x1, 4 x5. The model sees the Targets and the penalties
  but not the per-side effects, so it cannot price this; it is a
  question for the table, not for another run.
  The ladders themselves are still pitched with Standard as the
  in-band rung and Hard and Nobulous below it.
  BAT's Health dial was tuned against this model, so the model
  agreeing with the shipped ladder is construction, not
  corroboration. The rulebooks no longer print win rates at all -
  this file is the only place they live.

CAVEATS

  THE BAT TIER COLUMNS USED TO RUN THE WRONG WAY, and the reason is worth
  keeping. The tier modules made BAT EASIER - Tier II +6.1 and Tier III
  +7.2 against no-NRJ - which contradicted the intent that Tier II is the
  pressure peak. The caveat here blamed content the model omits. That was
  wrong.

  BAT's Tier II was the only tier in the set that ADDED cards to a deck.
  NAP replaces at every tier; BAT shuffled 6 Unauthorized Modifications
  INTO a 22-card Upgrade deck, so the Rats simply drew more Upgrades, and
  every Upgrade absorbs one Tyrant attack before it is spent. The tier was
  handing out armor as a side effect of its own delivery mechanism.

  Two card-design changes fixed it, with no difficulty dial touched:
    * the Modifications REPLACE 6 ordinary Upgrades (MODS_REPLACE)
    * their Values rose by 1, to 4/4/5/4/5/4

  The second is the counter-intuitive one. Splices come out of the Upgrade
  deck, so under threshold damage a card's Value is the height of a wall the
  Rats must clear in ONE blow - it matters more to the Tyrant than to the
  Rat holding it. LOWERING the Modification bodies made Tier II easier;
  raising them gave it teeth.

  THE EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOLS WERE REBALANCED, and how they were broken is
  worth keeping. Tier I used to measure about -1.0, and an earlier caveat
  blamed unmodeled content. That was wrong - all seven Tyrant Attack cards
  are handled individually, and borrowing is implemented including the
  banishment check and both Protocols that hook it. Forcing each Protocol in
  turn showed the real problem: the six cards disagreed violently, three
  making BAT HARDER and three EASIER, spanning 29 points. Tier I averaged to
  nothing because they cancelled.

  TWO ARE NOW NEAR-INERT, AND NEITHER CARD CHANGED. Accelerated Trials
  gates on printed Target 5+ and lost most of its deck when TARGET_DELTA

  it went -2.8 to -0.2 on the policy change alone. Both are design
  questions, and both illustrate the same thing: a card tuned against a
  policy is only as stable as the policy.

  TWO OF THEM GATE ON THE PRINTED TARGET, SO ZEROING TARGET_DELTA RE-AIMED
  THEM. Accelerated Trials fires at printed Target 5+ and Independent
  Funding at 4 or less. With the model's +1 still on top, the deck read
  4/4/5x13/6x5, so Accelerated Trials covered 18 of the 20 Experiments and
  Independent Funding 2. The printed deck is 3/3/4x13/5x5, which is 5 and
  15 - very nearly swapped. Accelerated Trials fell from -6.9 to -0.8 and
  is now the one card outside the -3 to -6 band, not because its text
  changed but because the Targets under it did. Raise it as a design
  question before touching the dial: ACCEL_FLOOR = 4 measures -9.9, which
  is as far outside the band on the other side.

  WHICH DIE DOES "EXHAUST AN AVAILABLE DIE" TAKE? It used to take the best
  one, in all three games, by accident. BAT walked rat.dice, which is COLORS
  order - purple, blue, green, yellow - and took the first available, so it
  handed over the PURPLE every time. NAP and AoS held one flat list ending
  in red and popped from the end, so they handed over the RED die first, the
  strongest in the game. AoS did it twice: allocate() sorts the pool
  strongest-first, so an element's dice run best-to-worst and dice[:burn]
  burned the best ones allocated to it.

  A player hands over the yellow, which makes those clauses nearly free -
  Accelerated Trials measured +4.5 once the model stopped overcharging it.
  So the three cards that exhaust dice as a cost now NAME a colour (Purple
  for Accelerated Trials and Hypermetabolism, Blue for Independent Funding),
  and NAP's Demand penalties name the Assignment's own recovery dot. Holding
  none of that colour is not a free pass: the weakest die goes instead.
  AoS's exhaust clauses carry no colour, so there the weakest simply goes.

  Correcting this moved NAP by +0.1 and AoS by +1.0; BAT's Protocols were
  retuned against it. If a cost clause seems inert, check WHICH die it takes
  before making it bigger.

  TWO OF THOSE TURNED ON TIMING, NOT SIZE. Accelerated Trials billed the
  active Rat's dice AFTER the attempt, when it has already spent them -
  raising the charge from 2 dice to 4 changed the win rate by nothing at
  all, because there was nothing left to take. Billing ONE die before the
  attempt was worth -5.0 when most of the deck was Target 5+. If a cost
  clause seems inert, check when it fires before making it bigger - and
  check what its trigger is gated on before trusting the figure later.

  BAT'S TIER III RECOVERED WEAKLY, AND THE REASON MOVED TWICE. Tier II is
  the trough as intended. Tier III gained only +4.7 back at Standard until
  the reward side was fixed on 2026-11-06; it gains +9.2 now, against NAP's
  +3.9 and AoS's +13.0. The first diagnosis was the claim side:
  two of the six Research Notes were nearly dead paper, and the rest
  averaged 2.0 claims a game. Loosening two conditions and rewriting the
  dead one - Cross-Species Compatibility now claims on completing an
  Experiment with a BORROWED die, which is what the card is about - took
  that to 4.3 claims a game, with each of the five modelled Notes claimed
  in 73-96% of games and only 0.1% of games claiming none. The sixth,
  Controlled Failure, needs a deliberate zero-dice turn the policy never
  takes.

  It barely moved the column, because the limit was the REWARD side. Until
  2026-11-06 bat.py pooled every claim into one counter, `self.notes`, and
  spent it on two of the six printed rewards. The other four did nothing at
  all. Six separate once-per-game cards were one fungible token spent on
  two of them - the same defect AoS's Backdoor Routines had.

  FIXED 2026-11-06, AND IT WAS WORSE THAN THE COUNT SUGGESTS. Of the two

  moves no published figure, because under the shipped trigger
  the branch still never fires.

  INDEPENDENT FUNDING NEEDED A NEW TRIGGER, not a new number. The old card
  paid the LENDER a die, and the lender's dice are not the binding resource,
  so it was worth +0.1 despite firing 2.7 times a game. Eight triggers were
  measured: anything at resolve time was worth 0.0 (the active Rat has
  already spent its dice), anything keyed to Upgrades held was dodged for
  free (the Rat-selection policy already prefers the least-loaded Rat), and
  anything undodgeable was far too big - a setup banish -11.0, taxing the Red
  holder -10.5, with nothing in between, because a Rat owns only 4 dice.

  It now taxes 1 die BEFORE each Experiment with printed Target 4 or lower -
  the complement of Accelerated Trials' Target 5+, so the two gate on
  opposite halves of the deck - and keeps the lender payout as its upside.
  Worth -5.4.

  WHAT DID NOT WORK IS THE MORE USEFUL HALF. Twenty-one candidates and
  combinations were measured; proto/fix.txt has them all.
  Every one that left the recovery floor fixed moved the win rate without
  creating any dice pressure whatever - including one that banishes eight of
  the seventeen dice per game and still reads 0% and 0.0%. "Recover up to
  REC exhausted dice" refills the pool to a fixed floor every attempt
  regardless of what happened, so while that holds, the pool cannot trend
  toward zero. Making failure destroy dice is the obvious fix and it does
  nothing. Letting the printed banish trade pay from the exhausted pile is
  worse than nothing: it wins 100%, because at 2 dice per Health with 17
  dice and 4 Health, dice are four times the cheaper currency and the trade
  is only balanced while it stays unpayable.

  THE ONE CLAUSE THAT WOULD MAKE DICE CURRENCY CANNOT BE PAID. The failure
  choice is resolved between the commit and the recovery - rules/nrj.html
  steps 4, 5, 6 - which is the trough. At that instant the Imposter holds
  0.11 dice on average, NONE AT ALL in 98% of failures, and can pay in dice
  in 2.1%. The retuned player commits more, so the trough is deeper and the
  clause is less payable than ever. The printed rule anticipates it ("if you cannot banish 2 available
  dice, Health loss is mandatory"), so the finale is a Health race with a
  dice floor. Same shape as BAT's Accelerated Trials: a cost billed after
  the spend finds an empty pool. Resolving the choice after recovery, or
  banishing from the exhausted pile, would make the trade real - both are
  rules changes and neither has been made.

  SIX SCENARIOS RESTRICT THE DICE, AND ARE WORTH -1.8 POINTS. "Commit at
  least 6 dice", "commit at least 3 different colors", the two color
  minimums and "Yellow cannot contribute" were never modelled; they are now,
  and so is every other Scenario effect bar one. Three of them block the
  MAJORITY of the attempts they appear in - 75%, 74%, 73% - and the whole
  set moves the finale 63.5% to 61.7%, because by the time they bite the
  attempt was already lost, and being blocked saves the dice and the
  Research cards it would have wasted.

  The three color clauses used to block 0% of their attempts, because
  recovering the best dice leaves a pool of Purple and Blue. Repair H gave
  two of them teeth: with the pool down to two or three dice, An Old Injury
  now blocks 33% of its attempts and The Ceremonial Seal 22%, because the
  pool may simply not hold the Purple the card asks for. The Darkened
  Corridor still blocks 0% - it bars YELLOW, and a pool recovered
  strongest-first holds no Yellow to bar. That one is inert as printed. They are a tax on the recovery decision - recover the strongest
  dice and you keep the Purple but lose the spread - which is a decision the
  finale did not previously contain.

  WHERE THE MARGIN WAS ZERO. BAT's Candidate supply is the Tyrant's 5
  Splices, and the track holds exactly 5. With 1 retained Upgrade that is 5
  needed against 5 available; a win with NO retained Upgrade needs 6 and
  comes up one short. It did not happen in any win measured, but the rules
  permit it and were silent on it. rules/bat.html now fills from unused
  Upgrade cards when the Splices do not cover it - the same source Splicing
  Machine Malfunction uses to top the Tyrant up to 5.

  The "at most 1 Specimen" cap is what binds, not supply: a second Specimen
  is on the table in 43-70% of wins and has to be left behind.

  EVERY DIAL IS STILL A CLIFF, AND THAT IS NOT FIXED. One point of starting
  Health is worth 19 to 41 points of win rate and one point on all 24
  Targets about 32, against roughly 6 for a point of Tyrant Health in BAT.
  The retune made this WORSE, not better: a player who commits two above
  the Target converts each point of Health into more wins, so the 4-to-5
  Health step alone is worth 41 points.
  Nine pass/fail attempts against a small failure budget amplify any dial
  about ninefold. Flattening that means grading each attempt instead of
  passing or failing it, which is a larger change than H.

     commit rule it says nothing, because there is no way to
     commit FOR a Crit.  EXAM_CRIT_GATE models the gate reading
     and ships off.  The clause wants rewording.

  4  THE DEVIOUS COUPONS ARE NAMED CARDS AGAIN.  ignore_setback()
     and move_setback() called .pop(), which takes whichever
     coupon is on the end, so a coupon earned by any of the six
     bought an ability none of them prints.  The three off-card
     levers now take a TUPLE OF COUPON NAMES rather than a
     boolean - a lever has to name the card paying for it - and
     all three ship empty.  audit.py reads 0% off-card against
     27% before.  The type change is the point: the fungible form
     can no longer be written by accident.

  5  REDUNDANT ORGANS IS PAID FOR WHAT IT PRINTS.  "The first
     time the holder would be knocked out, discard this card
     instead; the holder remains at 1 Health."  It fires in 12.6%
     of Tier II games and is worth about 0.0 points of win rate,
     because a Rat holding a Value-5 Upgrade is almost never
     reached in Health at all - only Crushing Blow's 1 direct
     damage gets past armor.  Implemented, reachable, firing, and
     not a balance lever: all four of those are findings.
       MOD_EFFECTS[4] STAYS ALL-ZERO AND IS RIGHT TO.
     damage_tyrant walks SURVIVING Splices only and a blow under
     a card's Value bounces off entirely, so Health is reached
     only once every Splice is destroyed - a Spliced Redundant
     Organs cannot be alive when the Tyrant would fall.  The card
     is unreachable on that side rather than unwritten.
       And drop_mod() is keyed on the CARD now instead of its
     Value.  Adrenal Gland and Redundant Organs both print Value
     5, and resolve_attack did not check that the Upgrade it lost
     was a Modification at all, so losing any Value-5 Upgrade
     discarded a Value-5 Mod with it.

  THREE MORE DEFECTS OF THE SAME FAMILY, found while fixing those
  five.  Each is a printed effect with no implementation and no
  error, which is the Unexpected Resilience shape.

  6  "BANISH 1 LIMITATION FROM THE GAME" DID NOTHING.  It is one
     of the six Program Reward names, printed on one Program of
     each deck, and Game.reward had no branch for it - so three
     of the 24 Programs paid out silently nothing.  coverage.py
     waved all three Program decks through at 8 of 8 because its
     predicate was len() of the reward table: A NAME IN A TABLE
     IS NOT AN IMPLEMENTATION, which is the exact lesson `fires`
     was written for, applied everywhere in that file except
     here.  The predicate now counts branches.

  7  THE SIX CORRUPTED LIMITATIONS, which the audit called the
     only deck in the set the model never drew.  Five constrain
     the ALLOCATION and this model builds the allocation
     explicitly, so they are applied to the plan after allocation
     and before the roll; Deprecated Function is a scoring
     corruption.  A die that has to come off an element goes back
     to the pool UNCOMMITTED, because none of these cards spends
     a die - Stack Overflow is the one that does and says so.
     audit.py now draws each at 16.6-16.7%.
       THE STAND-IN WAS CLOSE, AND GETTING THERE TOOK A SECOND
     LOOK AT THE IMPLEMENTATION.  Applied as written first -
     constraint checked against a plan built WITHOUT it, surplus
     dice simply deleted - the six cards read 27.4% at Standard
     against the stand-in's 33.6%, which would have made them
     savage.  That 3.5 points was a MODEL ARTEFACT: a player
     allocating under "the first element must receive more dice
     than the last" arranges the same dice differently rather
     than throwing three of them away, so corrupt_plan now MOVES
     a shed die to any element that will legally take it and only
     leaves it uncommitted when none will.  Rearranged, the six
     printed cards read 31.7% against the stand-in's 33.6% - 1.9
     points harsher, which is a well-calibrated replacement
     rather than a re-pitch.
       THE LESSON IS WORTH MORE THAN THE NUMBER.  An allocation
     constraint applied AFTER allocation is not the same card as
     one applied during it, and the difference ran the wrong way
     by twice the real effect.

  8  PRODIGY HAD NO ABILITY FOR THE WHOLE LIFE OF nap.py, and it
     is the clearest case in the set of a card that cannot be
     modelled by itself.  It prints "when using two dice of the
     same color as wild, only one of those dice must roll a
     success" - a relaxation of a rule the model did not have.
     rules/nap.html requires "a successful die matching its color
     dot, Red as wild, or two SUCCESSFUL dice of the same other
     color"; nap.py enforced that on the COMMIT and never asked
     whether those dice succeeded, which is strictly more
     generous than the cards.  So ABILITIES mapped Prodigy to
     None - and Prodigy is the default in nap.run and the
     Apprentice audit.py audits NAP with, so the default
     Apprentice was the one with nothing to do.
       COLOR_RULE_ON_SUCCESS puts the rule in.  Prodigy's ability
     is then worth +4.4 points, the largest of the six.

  AND THE FOUR TIER DECKS THAT HAD NO PER-CARD IDENTITY NOW HAVE
  ONE.  The four Imposter Tasks and the four Following Setbacks
  were a subject, a dot and two raised Targets; each now carries
  its printed effect.  nap.FOLLOWING was wrong three ways at
  once: a FLAT 1-die upkeep on all four when only Minor
  Laboratory Fire prints one, four interchangeable effects, and a
  dot drawn at random from PBGY when all four print one.  A
  Following Setback does not print the base card's row lock
  either - only Unscheduled Demonstration prints a weaker version
  of it - so the lock is now keyed on the card rather than on
  whether it happens to have an upkeep.  And the last two Tasks
  with no modelled text went in: both pay in DECK ORDER, which is
  why they sat out, and they are worth +1.2 points together.

    NAP   EVERYTHING EXCEPT THE COLOUR RULE IS NET NEUTRAL.  With

    NRJ   the finale pitch is unmoved to the decimal.

  THE THREE THAT REMAIN, and none of them is an oversight.  All
  three are printed rewards that pay in INFORMATION, in models
  with no decision that information would change.

    Diagnostic Trial (BAT)  "look at the next Experiment and
        RETURN IT TO THE TOP" - it cannot reorder anything, and
        the Experiment policy already commits the MINIMUM
        expected to reach the Target, so there is nothing to hold
        back on the strength of knowing what is coming.
    Packet Sniffer (AoS)    "look at the next Limitation before
        selecting a Program" - play() takes Programs off a
        shuffled queue because the rules deal them in order, so
        there is no selection, and the dice a Limitation
        withholds already leave the pool before this model
        allocates.
    Neural Response Screening (NAP)  not an information card but
        the other kind of gap: Assignment language on a Final
        Exam.  See 3 above.

  Modelling any of the three would mean inventing a decision the
  game does not offer, which is worse than a declared gap.  Each
  reason is recorded at the code, and coverage.py fails if the
  claim around it goes stale.
