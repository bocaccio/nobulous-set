6. THE RESEARCH POOL
   A card is retained or it is not, and every retained card takes
   2 off one Exhibit's Target. The spent/unspent split was dropped
   on 2026-10-30: nothing on the table records which a card is,
   and a campaign can sit for weeks between games. So the pool is
   now just a COUNT, and seven is the count that reads 50%.
   NAP/BAT/AoS retained        worth 1  worth 2
   1/1/1                          2.6%     6.6%
   2/2/1                          7.0%    21.4%
   2/2/2                         11.0%    34.2%
   3/2/2                         16.8%    48.7%   <- shipped
   3/3/2                         21.8%    58.8%
   3/3/3                         28.8%    71.0%
   no retained cards                       0.2%

   Eight cards worth 1.5 each beat six worth 2, on the same total
   reduction: spreading across more Exhibits pays better than
   concentrating, and more cards cover the three domains better.
   That is why dropping to one value needed the COUNTS re-derived
   rather than the values doubled.

8. THE DIFFICULTY LADDER - dice exhausted at setup
   The rules name the dice, so there is no choice to model. The
   counts are spread across the colours on purpose: taking whole
   ones collides with the wheel, which opens Yellow+Green, and
   '4 yellow 4 green' reads 28.7% against 38.4% for the same
   eight dice taken off the purple end. The red is never taken.
   AND SINCE 2026-11-16 THE DICE ARE NOT THE WHOLE DIAL. They
   stop at fourteen, because the attention opens Yellow+Green
   and a rung that takes every yellow AND every green opens
   with nothing it may apply - at sixteen you hold one red die
   and session 1 is skipped rather than hard. Every rung keeps
   a yellow and a green. Health carries the last two: a
   ceiling, not a point of damage, since recovery caps on it.

   AND WHETHER THE RUNGS ARE STILL GAMES OF SKILL. A tier where
   no policy beats another is not a harder puzzle, it is a worse
   one, so every rung is measured with its own gaps. This is the
   property that chose this dial: the RECOVERY decision, which is
   what the attention wheel exists to create, pays MORE as the
   ladder gets harder.
   CARDS is the gap this dial gained on 2026-11-16: how much
   scoring the hand beats spending whatever came up. It is the
   one lever that rests on a table this project invented, so
   section 9 tests it by dealing that table to the wrong cards.
   RESEARCH IS THE ONE THAT CHANGES SIGN, and a negative figure
   there is not a typo: it is how much spending the Pool at once
   would BEAT the policy this module plays at that rung. Holding
   is worth most at Easy and turns negative by Nobulous, so no
   single res is best on all four rungs - see THE LADDER in the
   header. Nothing exhausted is not a rung any more, so the
   +17.3 this is often quoted at is off the ladder now.

   WHICH CARD WAS NOT A DECISION THIS MODEL COULD FIND UNTIL
   2026-11-14, and it is the policy since 2026-11-16. Scoring
   the hand measured +0.4 against a Pool spent the session it
   was dealt and measures +5.0 against one held, and the spread
   between the best and worst single clause went from 2.6
   points to 4.8. Choosing WHEN gave choosing WHICH something
   to decide, which is what the clauses were printed for.
   See THE EIGHTEEN CLAUSES in the header, and audit.py for how
   often each one actually goes off.

   AND HOW MUCH OF THAT IS THE SCORING TABLE BEING RIGHT. This
   is the test the default rests on, because CLAUSE_WORTH is a
   guess this file wrote rather than a rule printed on
   anything. It deals the SAME eighteen scoring functions to the
   WRONG cards - the same numbers, no information - and re-reads
   the rung. If a permuted table kept the gain, the gain would
   be the act of scoring and the guess would not matter.
   dealt to the wrong cards #1          42.7%   -1.0
   dealt to the wrong cards #2          43.0%   -0.7
   dealt to the wrong cards #3          47.9%*  +4.1
   dealt to the wrong cards #4          44.8%   +1.0
   dealt to the wrong cards #5          41.5%   -2.2
      their mean                        44.0%   +0.2
   AGAINST +5.0 FOR THE TRUE TABLE. A permuted one is worth
   +0.2, and 3 of the 5 read WORSE than spending what came to
   hand. So the gain is the table's CONTENT, all of it, and the
   four printed rungs are pegged to it being approximately
   right. Every term in it reads something FACE-UP, which is
   what makes that bearable rather than what makes it untrue.

THE PROTOTYPE'S ERRATA - four finale10 claims that the real
components did not support:
  1 'the pace cannot be memorised'. The schedule rule IS the
    winning pace and it is one formula, so the depth is in
    execution. finale10 said as much in its honest-limit note;
    what is new is that its own throttled variant measures WORSE
    than the plain schedule here, 28.3% against 48.4%.
  2 'a three-colour window'. At three colours the finale reads
    67.4% and this module's own recovery policy is no longer the
    best one, so the window has to be TWO for the dial's central
    decision to pay at all.
  3 its pacing figures. `clock` rounds the schedule and so falls
    quietly behind at a pressure just over 1 - see how_many().
  4 'commit only the dice you need', its LARGEST lever at +35.1.
    Worth +13.4 here - and it is the lever most sensitive to the
    recovery economy. At REC 4 the gap is down to +1.4, and by
    REC 5 it INVERTS: throwing every allowed die wins by 4.6,
    which is why REC is 3 - the Rewards ARE the fourth point of
    recovery, and they have to be earned.
