Generated from Compendium v22 / AoS v10 / NAP v0.9c / BAT v0.6 / NRJ v0.24

CHANGES IN THIS REVISION
  - LADDER RE-PEGGED AGAINST THE PARENT GAME.  The 20 Strong community stats
    sheet (952 logged games) puts the published decks at Solar Sentinels 49%,
    Too Many Bones 54%, Hoplomachus: Victorum 53% - 49-54% over 577 games on the
    three flagship decks, 53% across all of them.  20 Strong is designed as a
    coin flip, and BAT's Standard was sitting 25 points above it.
        Easy      4 / 4   ~70%
        Standard  5 / 5   ~63%
        Hard      6 / 6   ~56%
        Brutal    7 / 7   ~49%
        Nobulous  8 / 8   ~42%
    Standard lands a little above the parent game on purpose: the simulator
    plays a near-optimal commitment policy every turn, so a human's first-play
    rate will sit below these numbers.
    Track gold spaces re-marked 4 to 8.  Eight Specimens is the ceiling, so past
    Nobulous only Buff HP rises; the track runs to 12, where the Tyrant wins
    about three games in four.
  - The full difficulty surface, with the NRJ tiers interleaving between rungs:
                      no NRJ   Tier I   Tier II   Tier III
        Easy     4     70.4%    68.9%    62.6%     68.2%
        Standard 5     62.6%    60.9%    55.1%     60.9%
        Hard     6     55.7%    54.0%    47.7%     55.9%
        Brutal   7     47.6%    46.4%    40.7%     49.1%
        Nobulous 8     42.1%    40.0%    36.3%     44.4%
    Tier II is worth about -6 to -7, so it sits roughly midway between two
    difficulty numbers - fifteen usable settings from 70% down to 36%.
  - Tier III campaign: finale 73.2% with BAT at Standard, BAT replays 0.66.

EARLIER (rev 26)
  - RED IN THE BATTLE IS A TRADE, NOT A TIMER.  It is always available to the
    attacking Rat and committed on top of the six pool dice, free and without
    exhausting - but ONLY if that Rat does not tap an Upgrade that round.
    Worth only +2 to +2.8 points, against +6 to +12 for a freely available Red.
    A tap is usually more raw damage (Value-1, so 1 to 4) than Red's 7/6, so Red
    becomes the weapon of a Rat with nothing left to tap.
    Two ways of playing the choice were measured - purely for damage (Red on
    5-11% of rounds) and to protect armour (30-49%) - and they land WITHIN ONE
    POINT of each other at every rung.  The rule is robust to how well it is
    played, which is worth more than either number.
    Rejected on measurement: Red pinned to the leftmost undamaged Splice until
    that Splice dies (the row dies fast, so Red returns on 77% of rounds - more
    generous than a cooldown, not less); pinned to the rightmost (74% falling to
    57%, never scarce, extra rule to explain); every-other-round (55%, worth
    +6 to +12 and needs the whole ladder moved).
  - LADDER BACK TO CONSECUTIVE NUMBERS:
        Easy 2/2 ~85%   Standard 3/3 ~78%   Hard 4/4 ~70%
        Brutal 5/5 ~63%   Nobulous 6/6 ~55%
    Even 7-8 point steps, and it extends: 7 is about 49%, 8 about 42%.
  - Tier III campaign: 69.1% with BAT at Standard, 73.1% at Brutal.

EARLIER (rev 25)
  - RED PLAYS IN THE TYRANT BATTLE, exactly as it does in the Splice Phase.  It
    was never swept into the shared pool because the simulator stores Red as a
    flag on whoever holds it rather than as one of that Rat's dice - an artefact,
    not a decision.  The attacking Rat now commits Red if it holds it, on top of
    the six pool dice, then sets it aside and places it on a non-attacking Rat,
    so it returns about every other round.  It never costs a die from the pool.
    Worth +6 to +12 points, growing with difficulty: it cannot miss, it is free,
    and it does not move the countdown.  (Folding Red into the pool as an
    ordinary die was tested and is worth only +2; counting it against the
    six-dice cap costs about a point against committing it on top.)
  - LADDER RE-PEGGED.  Still one number used twice, but the numbers move up:
        Easy      3 / 3   ~85%
        Standard  4 / 4   ~79%
        Hard      5 / 5   ~72%
        Brutal    7 / 7   ~59%
        Nobulous  8 / 8   ~53%
    Eight is the ceiling - there are only eight Specimen cards.
  - STAT DICE ASSIGNED.  The Strategy die marks the active Lab Rat in both
    phases.  The Health die runs the Tyrant track.  The Recovery die is not used.
  - Tier III campaign: 73.8% with BAT at Standard, 68.8% at Brutal-ish (N=6).

EARLIER (rev 24)
  - SPLICES ARE A FACE-UP STACK DURING PLAY, SHUFFLED INTO A ROW AT THE BATTLE.
    Reverts rev 23's build-as-you-go track.  At the battle: lay the Buff HP card
    with the Health die on the difficulty number, shuffle the five Splices into
    a row beside it, then set the Health dial to 20 at the right-hand end.
    The reason is shape, not balance.  Splice order and every sorted arrangement
    sit within about a point of each other, but the Upgrade deck is richest at
    the start, so Splices arrive big first - first Spliced averages Value 3.89,
    fifth 2.94.  Left in that order the Tyrant leads with its heaviest card and
    tapers, so every fight peaks in round one.  Shuffling removes the pattern.
  - The difficulty table is unchanged: it was measured with a shuffled row.
        Easy 2/2 ~84%   Standard 3/3 ~76%   Hard 4/4 ~69%
        Brutal 5/5 ~61%   Nobulous 6/6 ~53%

EARLIER (rev 23)
  - THE TYRANT TRACK IS BUILT DURING PLAY, NOT AT THE BATTLE.  At setup the Buff
    HP card goes beside the Tyrant Attack deck with the Health die on the
    difficulty number; every Splice is then laid in the rightmost open spot.  At
    5 Splices the Health dial is set to 20 and placed at the right-hand end.
    The row is therefore Buff HP, the Splices in the order they were spliced,
    Health - no sorting and no shuffling step at all.
  - Measured: splice order is NOT arbitrary.  The Upgrade deck is richest at the
    start, so the first card Spliced averages Value 3.89 and the fifth 2.94.
    The Tyrant leads with its heaviest Splice, which hits hardest and dies
    first, and that is worth +0.4 to +1.5 points to the Rats against a shuffled
    row.  Inside the ladder's tolerance, so no re-peg: the difficulty table
    stands at 84 / 76 / 69 / 61 / 53 percent.

EARLIER (rev 22)

        Easy      2 / 2   ~84%
        Standard  3 / 3   ~76%
        Hard      4 / 4   ~69%
        Brutal    5 / 5   ~61%
        Nobulous  6 / 6   ~53%

EARLIER (rev 21)
  - THE SIMULATOR WAS USING A FICTIONAL UPGRADE DECK.  It generated regular
    Upgrades as random Values 2-3 and Specimens as random 3-4.  The printed deck
    is fixed, has categories, and EVERY SPECIMEN IS VALUE 5:
        Power     Serrated Claws 2, Reinforced Jaw 3, Capacitor Muscles 3,
                  Piercing Horn 4
        Mobility  Spring-Loaded Legs 2, Prehensile Tail 2, Tripwire Tendons 3,
                  Evasive Reflexes 3
        Defense   Thickened Hide 2, Layered Carapace 3, Chromatic Plating 3,
                  Reactive Shell 4
        Senses    Acute Hearing 2, Predatory Focus 3, Rapid Metabolism 2,
                  Neural Overclock 4
        Specimens all 8 are Value 5
    Every BAT figure before rev 21 was measured against the invented deck.  The
    real one is stronger for the Rats: mean regular Value 2.81 against 2.5, and
    a Value 5 Specimen is superb all-or-nothing armour in a Rat's hands.
    'Defense' is now read from the card's category instead of being guessed from
    an even Value.
  - LADDER RE-PEGGED on the corrected deck:
        Easy      2 Specimens   Buff 0   ~89%
        Standard  3 Specimens   Buff 2   ~80%
        Hard      4 Specimens   Buff 4   ~69%
        Brutal    4 Specimens   Buff 6   ~60%
        Nobulous  5 Specimens   Buff 8   ~50%
    Nobulous takes a fifth Specimen because 4 Specimens at Buff 8 lands at 52%
    and leaves only a 6-point step from Brutal.
  - SPLICE ROW ORDER MEASURED AND IT BARELY MATTERS.  Defense first, fully
    random, by type, and by type-then-Value all sit within 2 points of each
    other.  Only lowest-Value-first is meaningfully worse for the Rats: it keeps
    the Tyrant's largest Splice alive longest, so its dice count stays up
    (2.80 dice an attack against 2.70).  Defense first is kept as the printed
    rule on thematic grounds; the rulebook says the rest is the player's choice.
  - Tier III campaign: 76.9% with BAT at Standard, 70.3% at Hard.  The Standard
    figure has drifted about 5 points above the 8/9/10 finale calibration
    because the corrected deck banks richer BAT carryover (retained cards 2.38,
    retained Specimens 0.66).  Flagged, not yet re-tuned.

EARLIER (rev 19)
  - RAT UPGRADES NO LONGER TAKE ACCUMULATED DAMAGE.  A Tyrant attack either meets
    an Upgrade's Value and destroys it, with the excess carrying on under the
    same test, or it cannot meet any remaining Upgrade and stops there.  No Rat
    card is ever part-damaged.  The Tyrant's Splices still accumulate, tracked
    by the Health die walking its row.
    Measured across 60,000 battles: the worst case anywhere on the table is ONE
    partially-damaged Tyrant Splice and ZERO Rat Upgrades, so the Health die
    covers the entire battle and the game needs no tokens, counters or notes -
    just the 20 Strong dice, the cards and the Chip.
  - BUFF HP LADDER RE-PEGGED TO 0 / 3 / 6 / 9 / 12, using the 12-space track:
        Easy      2 Specimens   Buff  0   ~89%
        Standard  3 Specimens   Buff  3   ~79%
        Hard      4 Specimens   Buff  6   ~66%
        Brutal    4 Specimens   Buff  9   ~56%
        Nobulous  4 Specimens   Buff 12   ~45%
    Even 10-13 point steps, and the gold spaces on the track are 3, 6, 9, 12.
  - Tier III campaign re-run: finale 71.2% with BAT at Standard, 72.5% at Hard.
    Unchanged.

EARLIER (rev 18)
  - THE TYRANT NOW USES ITS SPLICES.  Its FRONTMOST Splice applies its effect on
    every Tyrant attack - free, automatic, no tapping - and it uses the card's
    PRINTED Value, so damage already marked on that card does not weaken it.
    Until now the Tyrant's Splices were pure armour and their printed effects
    were never used at all.  Modelled as the Rats' own tap abstraction, Value-1
    extra damage, this costs the Rats 4-10 points and the cost grows with
    difficulty.  A 'tap' variant was tested and rejected: with no cost to
    tapping, the Tyrant would tap every round and its frontmost Splice could
    never be destroyed.  Reading past Defense Splices instead of using the
    frontmost measured identically, so the simple rule wins.
  - BUFF HP IS ONE CARD AGAIN, a 12-space track.  Put the Health die on the
    difficulty's number and step it down one space per damage.  The Health die
    also walks the Splice row before that, sitting on whichever card is being
    damaged - one die runs the entire Tyrant, and the dial and the second card
    are both gone.
  - THE ORIGINAL BUFF HP LADDER IS RESTORED.  Random Tyrant dice gave the Rats
    about +8 points and the frontmost-Splice effect takes about -8 back, so the
    table reads exactly as first published:
        Easy      2 Specimens   Buff 0   ~87%
        Standard  3 Specimens   Buff 2   ~77%
        Hard      4 Specimens   Buff 4   ~66%
        Brutal    4 Specimens   Buff 6   ~60%
        Nobulous  4 Specimens   Buff 8   ~50%
    At 3 Specimens the curve is 84 / 81 / 77 / 73 / 69 / 64 / 60 / 57 / 52 for
    Buff 0-8, within a point of the original figures at every rung.
  - Tier III campaign re-run end to end: finale 73.1% against a 72.8% baseline,
    retained cards 2.25 against 2.23.  Unchanged.

EARLIER (rev 17)
  - TYRANT DICE FIX.  The Tyrant commits a number of dice equal to the highest
    Value among the Splices it still holds; it now draws them AT RANDOM from
    its pool of 2 Purple / 2 Blue / 1 Green / 1 Yellow.  Previously the
    simulator took them strongest-first, and because Upgrade Values stop at 4
    and most attacks come after the biggest Splices are destroyed, it rolled
    Purple 89.8 percent of the time and Green or Yellow NEVER.  Two thirds of
    the printed pool had never been simulated.
    Blind drawing costs the Tyrant about 18 percent of its damage per attack
    (2.19 -> 1.79 expected successes) and is worth +5 to +11 points to the Rats,
    so the whole Buff HP ladder is re-pegged below.
  - BUFF HP LADDER RE-PEGGED, and BAT_BuffHP is now a 2-CARD deck.
        Easy      2 Specimens   Buff 2     ~86%
        Standard  3 Specimens   Buff 4     ~76%
        Hard      4 Specimens   Buff 6     ~67%
        Brutal    4 Specimens   Buff 8     ~58%

BUFF HP AND DIFFICULTY
  Difficulty is one number, used twice: that many Specimens in the Upgrade deck
  and that much Buff HP on the Tyrant.  Easy 4, Standard 5, Hard 6, Brutal 7,
  Nobulous 8, for 70 / 63 / 56 / 49 / 42 percent.
  Buff HP sits at the FRONT of the Tyrant's damage row, ahead of its Splices.
  BAT_BuffHP is a single one-sided track card with 12 spaces, gold at 4-8.  Put
  the Health die on the difficulty number when the battle starts; it steps one
  space per point of damage, then walks down the Splice row and onto Health.

DICE
  Use the 20 Strong Dice and Chip saved object: 4 Purple, 4 Blue, 4 Green,
  4 Yellow, 1 Red, plus the Health, Strategy and Recovery stat dice.

REV 28 — RULEBOOK EDIT
----------------------
Removed design rationale from all four rulebooks.  The rulebooks now state only
how to play; no explanation of why a rule is the way it is.  Deleted or trimmed:

BAT   - "The NRJ tiers sit between these rungs..." (difficulty table note)
      - "Why a separate track" callout box (Buff-HP-first rationale) — deleted
      - "at 12 the Tyrant wins about three games in four" (win-rate aside)
      - closing clause of "Two kinds of armor": "That is why the whole battle
        runs on one die: the Tyrant is a slab you grind down..."
NAP   - closing clause of "Special Recovery": "Choosing which card becomes
        Primary is therefore choosing which colour you get back in bulk..."
      - End of Day: "The apprenticeship is finite: work you never took is work
        that disappears."
      - exam pool: "Neglected subjects are thinly represented but brutal..."
        and "Which exams you faced is a direct record of which subjects..."
AoS   - closing clause of "Dice come back unless you are punished": "Exhaustion
        in AoS is a penalty, not a cost, which is why the gradient is the whole
