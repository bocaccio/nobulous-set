EARLIER (rev 26)
  - RED IN THE BATTLE IS A TRADE, NOT A TIMER.  It is always available to the
    attacking Rat and committed on top of the six pool dice, free and without
    exhausting - but ONLY if that Rat does not tap an Upgrade that round.
    Worth only +2 to +2.8 points, against +6 to +12 for a freely available Red.
    A tap is usually more raw damage (Value-1, so 1 to 4) than Red's 7/6, so Red
    becomes the weapon of a Rat with nothing left to tap.
    Two ways of playing the choice were measured - purely for damage (Red on
    5-11% of rounds) and to protect armor (30-49%) - and they land WITHIN ONE
    POINT of each other at every rung.  The rule is robust to how well it is
    played, which is worth more than either number.
    Rejected on measurement: Red pinned to the leftmost undamaged Splice until
    that Splice dies (the row dies fast, so Red returns on 77% of rounds - more
    generous than a cooldown, not less); pinned to the rightmost (74% falling to
    57%, never scarce, extra rule to explain); every-other-round (55%, worth
    +6 to +12 and needs the whole ladder moved).
  - LADDER BACK TO CONSECUTIVE NUMBERS:
        Easy 2/2 ~85%   Standard 3/3 ~78%   Hard 4/4 ~70%
        Brutal 5/5 ~63%   Nobulous 6/6 ~55%
    Even 7-8 point steps, and it extends: 7 is about 49%, 8 about 42%.
  - Tier III campaign: 69.1% with BAT at Standard, 73.1% at Brutal.

EARLIER (rev 25)
  - RED PLAYS IN THE TYRANT BATTLE, exactly as it does in the Splice Phase.  It
    was never swept into the shared pool because the simulator stores Red as a
    flag on whoever holds it rather than as one of that Rat's dice - an artefact,
    not a decision.  The attacking Rat now commits Red if it holds it, on top of
    the six pool dice, then sets it aside and places it on a non-attacking Rat,
    so it returns about every other round.  It never costs a die from the pool.
    Worth +6 to +12 points, growing with difficulty: it cannot miss, it is free,
    and it does not move the countdown.  (Folding Red into the pool as an
    ordinary die was tested and is worth only +2; counting it against the
    six-dice cap costs about a point against committing it on top.)
  - LADDER RE-PEGGED.  Still one number used twice, but the numbers move up:
        Easy      3 / 3   ~85%

EARLIER (rev 24)
  - SPLICES ARE A FACE-UP STACK DURING PLAY, SHUFFLED INTO A ROW AT THE BATTLE.
    Reverts rev 23's build-as-you-go track.  At the battle: lay the Buff HP card
    with the Health die on the difficulty number, shuffle the five Splices into
    a row beside it, then set the Health dial to 20 at the right-hand end.
    The reason is shape, not balance.  Splice order and every sorted arrangement
    sit within about a point of each other, but the Upgrade deck is richest at
    the start, so Splices arrive big first - first Spliced averages Value 3.89,
    fifth 2.94.  Left in that order the Tyrant leads with its heaviest card and
    tapers, so every fight peaks in round one.  Shuffling removes the pattern.
  - The difficulty table is unchanged: it was measured with a shuffled row.
        Easy 2/2 ~84%   Standard 3/3 ~76%   Hard 4/4 ~69%
        Brutal 5/5 ~61%   Nobulous 6/6 ~53%

EARLIER (rev 23)
  - THE TYRANT TRACK IS BUILT DURING PLAY, NOT AT THE BATTLE.  At setup the Buff
    HP card goes beside the Tyrant Attack deck with the Health die on the
    difficulty number; every Splice is then laid in the rightmost open spot.  At
    5 Splices the Health dial is set to 20 and placed at the right-hand end.
    The row is therefore Buff HP, the Splices in the order they were spliced,
    Health - no sorting and no shuffling step at all.
  - Measured: splice order is NOT arbitrary.  The Upgrade deck is richest at the
    start, so the first card Spliced averages Value 3.89 and the fifth 2.94.
    The Tyrant leads with its heaviest Splice, which hits hardest and dies
    first, and that is worth +0.4 to +1.5 points to the Rats against a shuffled
    row.  Inside the ladder's tolerance, so no re-peg: the difficulty table
    stands at 84 / 76 / 69 / 61 / 53 percent.

EARLIER (rev 19)
  - RAT UPGRADES NO LONGER TAKE ACCUMULATED DAMAGE.  A Tyrant attack either meets
    an Upgrade's Value and destroys it, with the excess carrying on under the
    same test, or it cannot meet any remaining Upgrade and stops there.  No Rat

EARLIER (rev 18)
  - THE TYRANT NOW USES ITS SPLICES.  Its FRONTMOST Splice applies its effect on
    every Tyrant attack - free, automatic, no tapping - and it uses the card's
    PRINTED Value, so damage already marked on that card does not weaken it.
    Until now the Tyrant's Splices were pure armor and their printed effects
    were never used at all.  Modeled as the Rats' own tap abstraction, Value-1
    extra damage, this costs the Rats 4-10 points and the cost grows with
    difficulty.  A 'tap' variant was tested and rejected: with no cost to
    tapping, the Tyrant would tap every round and its frontmost Splice could
    never be destroyed.  Reading past Defense Splices instead of using the
    frontmost measured identically, so the simple rule wins.
  - BUFF HP IS ONE CARD AGAIN, a 12-space track.  Put the Health die on the
    difficulty's number and step it down one space per damage.  The Health die
    also walks the Splice row before that, sitting on whichever card is being
    damaged - one die runs the entire Tyrant, and the dial and the second card
    are both gone.

THE TYRANT, CURRENT
  Three things, in the order damage chews through them: an OUTER LAYER, the
  five SPLICES, and a CORE HEALTH.  The core cannot be touched until the layer
  and every Splice are gone.  The layer and the core are independent stats and
  neither is a slice of the other; rules/bat.html is the owner.
  Difficulty sets Specimens and core Health - Easy 5/9, Standard 6/10, Hard
  7/12, Nobulous 8/14, for 63.7 / 52.8 / 37.8 / 25.8 percent at 3000 games,
  no tier.  Standard is inside the 20 Strong community band on purpose.
  The OUTER LAYER IS 0 AT EVERY RUNG and is a fine-tuning dial the player
  raises; one point of it costs the Rats 4 to 8 points of win rate, so it is
  not a number to set casually.  simulation/price_buff.py prints the price
  list, and simulation/bat.py's damage_tyrant is the only place that decides
  the order.
  BAT_BuffHP is a single one-sided 15-space track card with no gold spaces -
  the difficulty table owns the value, so nothing on the card can mark a
  starting space without going stale.  The layer CHIPS: a blow takes what it
  can and stops, and only a blow that finishes the layer in the same attack
  reaches a Splice.  A Splice never chips.

NAP ASSIGNMENTS, CURRENT
  Revised 2026-11-12.  Three changes to all 26 Tasks at once, and none of them
  works alone.
    1. NO REWARD ASKS A QUESTION.  Fifteen Request effects were conditioned on
       how the dice fell - "If Exact", "If completed using exactly 3 dice", "If
       completed with exactly 2 successes across all committed dice this
       strategy phase" - which a player has to reconstruct after the dice are
       swept.  Every reward now pays on completion.
    2. THE REWARD NAMES A COLOUR, not "1 committed die".  Every fixed-colour
       REQUEST recovery pays a colour OTHER than the card's own dot, because
       the dot is what the Primary already queues for the next slot;
       check_app_sync.py enforces that, both halves of a two-colour offer
       included.  A DEMAND recovery may match its own dot on purpose - that is
       the die you just spent clearing it.
    3. BOTH FACES PAY.  A Demand was a harsher rule, a penalty one slot later
       and nothing for clearing it.  All 26 Demand faces print a completion
       reward now, so a deliberate flip is a decision rather than a failure.
       A Task pays at most once, on the face it was completed on.
  SIX DEMAND TARGETS CAME DOWN FROM 9 TO 6 - Align the Memory Probes,
  Recalibrate the Bone Saw, Prime the Synaptic Pump, Verify the Identity
  Matrix, Suture the Donor Incision, Dispose of Failed Samples - and Suture's
  Request rose 5 to 6.  Those six were the whole of the gap-4 and gap-3 tail
  the 2026-10-28 Target rise left behind, and a reward printed on a face
  nobody clears is dead text.  The widest Request-to-Demand gap is now 1, on
  10 of the 26; the other 16 print the same number on both faces.
  THE SIX OPPORTUNITIES EACH PRINT A WINDOW, before or after a roll, which is
  most of what a coupon is worth.  Study Nobulous' Notes changed job: it flips
  a Demand back to its Request side, keeping stored dice and progress, which
  is the only effect in NAP that undoes a flip.  Raid the Supply Closet is a
  recovery rather than a wild, and Borrow Better Instruments is a free reroll
  that does not spend a Strategy roll.
  THE FINAL EXAM POOL IS 1 CARD PER SUBJECT, chosen at random from each of the
  four, shuffled, three of the four drawn.  Every subject has a 75% chance to
  appear and can appear at most once; Credits only reduce Targets now.  It
  replaced a Credit-weighted pool that two of the four owners implemented by
  taking the FIRST cards of each subject in table order, so eight of the twelve
  printed exams could not come up at all on an empty transcript.  That is a
  real difficulty rise and the measured cost is in rules/RULES_CHANGELOG.txt.
